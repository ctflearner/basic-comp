Task 1: Password Audit — Check and Fix Your Passwords

Instructions

You are going to check how strong your current passwords are and replace the weak ones.

Step 1: List Your Accounts (15 min)

Write down EVERY online account you have (do NOT write the actual passwords). Use this table format:

# Account Password Length Reused? Common Word? Has Numbers/Symbols? Strong?
1 Gmail ? characters Yes/No Yes/No Yes/No Yes/No
2 Instagram ? characters Yes/No Yes/No Yes/No Yes/No
3 Facebook ? characters Yes/No Yes/No Yes/No Yes/No
4 WhatsApp (N/A — PIN)
5 Amazon ? characters Yes/No Yes/No Yes/No Yes/No

Add ALL accounts: banking apps, shopping sites, college portals, streaming services, gaming accounts, etc.

Step 2: Identify Weak Passwords (5 min)

Mark a password as WEAK if ANY of these are true:
- Less than 10 characters
- Used on more than one account (reused)
- Contains a common word (your name, "password", "123456")
- Contains only lowercase letters (no numbers, symbols, or uppercase)
- Contains personal info someone could guess (birthday, pet name, college name)

Step 3: Create New Passwords (10 min)

For every weak password, create a new strong one using the passphrase method:

  1. Think of a sentence: "I ate 3 samosas at the college canteen!"
  2. Turn it into a password: I@te3Samosas@Canteen!
  3. It is 20 characters, has uppercase, lowercase, numbers, and symbols
  4. It is easy for YOU to remember but impossible for others to guess

Create a new strong password for every weak account.

Step 4: Install Bitwarden (optional but recommended)

  1. Go to bitwarden.com and create a free account
  2. Install the Chrome browser extension
  3. Add your accounts and new passwords to Bitwarden
  4. Set a very strong master password (16+ characters, passphrase)

Expected Result

  • A completed audit table for all your accounts (at least 8 accounts listed)
  • Weak passwords identified
  • New strong passwords created for every weak account
  • (Optional) Bitwarden set up with at least 5 accounts saved

Bonus Challenge

  • Use Bitwarden's password generator to create random passwords for less important accounts
  • Check if your email has been in a data breach: go to haveibeenpwned.com, enter your email — if it appears in breaches, change that password immediately
  • Set up a routine: every 3 months, review and update your passwords

Task 2: Enable Two-Factor Authentication (2FA) on 3 Accounts

Instructions

Step 1: Install Google Authenticator
1. Open Play Store (Android) or App Store (iPhone)
2. Search for "Google Authenticator"
3. Install the official app by Google

Step 2: Enable 2FA on Gmail/Google Account
1. Open Chrome and go to myaccount.google.com
2. Click "Security" in the left menu
3. Under "Signing in to Google," click "2-Step Verification"
4. Click "Get started"
5. Google will ask you to verify with your phone number first (SMS)
6. After SMS verification, click "Authenticator app" > "Set up"
7. Choose your phone type (Android/iPhone) and click "Next"
8. Open Google Authenticator app on your phone
9. Tap the "+" button and select "Scan QR code"
10. Scan the QR code shown on your computer screen
11. The app will show a 6-digit code that changes every 30 seconds
12. Enter the code on your computer and click "Verify"
13. 2FA is now active on your Google account

Step 3: Enable 2FA on Instagram
1. Open Instagram app
2. Go to Profile > Settings (hamburger menu icon) > Accounts Center > Password and security
3. Tap "Two-factor authentication"
4. Select your Instagram account
5. Choose "Authentication app" (not SMS)
6. Instagram will show a key or QR code
7. Open Google Authenticator > tap "+" > scan the code or enter the key
8. Enter the 6-digit code from the Authenticator app into Instagram
9. Save the recovery codes Instagram gives you (write them down or screenshot)

Step 4: Enable 2FA on One More Account

Choose one of these:
- WhatsApp: Settings > Account > Two-step verification > Enable > set a 6-digit PIN
- Facebook: Settings > Security and Login > Two-Factor Authentication > Authenticator App
- Twitter/X: Settings > Security and Account Access > Security > Two-factor authentication
- Amazon: Account > Login & security > Two-Step Verification

Expected Result

  • Google Authenticator app installed with at least 2 accounts added
  • Gmail/Google account has 2FA enabled
  • Instagram has 2FA enabled
  • One additional account has 2FA enabled
  • Recovery codes saved in a safe place (not on your phone — write on paper and keep safe)

Bonus Challenge

  • Enable 2FA on ALL accounts that support it (check every major app you use)
  • Write down all recovery codes and store them in a sealed envelope at home (if you lose your phone, these codes let you get back into your accounts)
  • Check your bank app — does it have biometric login (fingerprint)? Enable it if available

Task 3: Privacy Settings Checkup

Instructions

Go through privacy settings on your major accounts and tighten them. Document every change you make.

Step 1: Instagram Privacy (10 min)

Open Instagram > Settings > Privacy. Make these changes:

Setting Where to Find Change To
Account Privacy Privacy > Account Privacy Private (toggle on)
Activity Status Privacy > Activity Status Off
Story Sharing Privacy > Story > Allow Sharing Off (or "Close Friends" only)
Message Requests Privacy > Messages "Your followers on Instagram"
Comments Privacy > Comments Limit to "People you follow"
Tags Privacy > Tags Manually Approve Tags (toggle on)

Step 2: Facebook Privacy (10 min)

Open Facebook > Settings > Privacy. Make these changes:

Setting Where to Find Change To
Who can see your posts Privacy Settings > Your Activity Friends
Friend requests Privacy Settings > How People Find You Friends of Friends
Phone number lookup Privacy Settings > How People Find You Friends (or Only Me)
Profile lock Privacy Checkup > Profile Lock Locked
Search engine indexing Privacy Settings Off (do not let search engines link to your profile)

Step 3: Phone App Permissions (10 min)

Go to phone Settings > Apps > App Permissions (or Privacy > Permission Manager on some phones).

Review these permissions and remove unnecessary access:

Permission Apps That Should Have It Remove From
Camera Camera app, WhatsApp, Instagram, Video calls Games, calculators, utilities
Microphone Phone, WhatsApp, Instagram, Meet Games, shopping apps
Location Maps, ride-sharing (Ola/Uber), food delivery Social media (or set to "Only while using"), games
Contacts Phone, WhatsApp, Email Shopping apps, games, utilities
Storage File manager, Camera, WhatsApp Apps that do not need to access your files

Step 4: Google Privacy Checkup (10 min)

  1. Go to myaccount.google.com/privacycheckup
  2. Walk through each section
  3. Key changes:
  4. Web & App Activity: Consider pausing (or set to auto-delete after 3 months)
  5. Location History: Turn off or set to auto-delete
  6. YouTube History: Set to auto-delete after 3 months
  7. Ad personalization: Review and consider turning off

Document your changes: Write down every setting you changed in a table format.

Expected Result

  • Instagram set to private with limited message access
  • Facebook privacy tightened (friends only)
  • At least 5 unnecessary app permissions removed on your phone
  • Google Privacy Checkup completed
  • A written record of all changes made

Bonus Challenge

  • Check LinkedIn privacy settings (Profile > Settings > Visibility)
  • Review WhatsApp privacy (Settings > Privacy — last seen, profile photo, about, status)
  • Uninstall apps you no longer use — they may still have permissions and access to your data

Task 4: Phishing Identification Test

Instructions

Your instructor will describe 5 emails/messages below. For EACH one, determine:
1. Is it LEGITIMATE or PHISHING?
2. List every red flag you notice (or explain why it is safe)
3. What should you do?

- Location: Lagos, Nigeria
- Device: Chrome on Windows

Google blocked this sign-in attempt. If this was you, you can review your security activity. If this was not you, change your password immediately.

[Review Activity]  [Change Password]

You received this because you turned on security alerts.

Paytm Rewards Team

For Each Email, Write:

  1. Verdict: Legitimate or Phishing?

  • Correct identification of phishing vs legitimate emails
  • At least 3 specific red flags identified for each phishing email
  • Appropriate recommended actions for each scenario

Bonus Challenge

  • Create your own fake phishing email example and challenge a classmate to identify the red flags.

Task 5: Real-Life Project — "My Digital Security Plan"

Instructions

Create YOUR personal digital security plan — a document you will actually follow to stay safe online. This is not a theoretical exercise; this is YOUR real security plan.

Write a 1-page document (in Google Docs or Word) with these sections:

Section 1: My Password Strategy

Write down your personal password rules:
- Minimum password length you will use
- What method you will use (passphrase, password manager, or both)
- How often you will change passwords
- Name of the password manager you will use (if any)
- How you will store recovery codes

Section 2: Two-Factor Authentication Status

Create a table:

Account 2FA Enabled? Method (App/SMS/PIN) Recovery Codes Saved?
Gmail Yes/No Yes/No
Instagram Yes/No Yes/No
WhatsApp Yes/No Yes/No
Banking App Yes/No Yes/No
(add more)

Section 3: My Backup Plan

  • What files are most important to me? (list them)
  • Where will I back them up? (Google Drive, external drive, both?)
  • How often will I back up? (weekly, monthly?)
  • When will I test my backups?

Section 4: My Privacy Settings

Confirm you have completed these:
- [ ] Instagram set to private
- [ ] Facebook privacy tightened
- [ ] Unnecessary app permissions removed
- [ ] Google Privacy Checkup completed
- [ ] Location sharing limited

Section 5: If I Get Hacked — My Emergency Plan

Write the exact steps you will take, in order:
1. First I will...
2. Then I will...
3. Then I will...
(Include: which password to change first, who to call, what to check)

Section 6: My Cybersecurity Learning Goals (Optional)

If cybersecurity interests you as a career:
- What area interests me most?
- What is my first learning step?
- What free resource will I start with?
- What is my 6-month goal?

Expected Result

  • A complete, personal digital security plan (saved as a Google Doc or Word file)
  • All sections filled with YOUR specific information
  • Actionable steps that you will actually follow
  • This document becomes your reference for staying safe online

Bonus Challenge

  • Share your security plan with a family member and help them create their own
  • Set a recurring Google Calendar reminder: "Monthly Security Checkup" — review passwords, check for breaches, update software
  • Create a simple presentation (3-5 slides) about cybersecurity basics that you could present to your family
