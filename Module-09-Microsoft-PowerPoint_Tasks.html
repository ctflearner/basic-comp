Module 9: Tasks & Assignments -- Mastering Microsoft PowerPoint

How to Use This File

Complete each task in order. Each task builds on skills from the previous one. Save ALL your completed presentations -- you will reference them for practice and your portfolio. Every task works on any Windows 11 computer with Microsoft PowerPoint (2019 or Microsoft 365).

Task 1: "About Me" Presentation (5 Slides)

Difficulty: Beginner
Time: 25 minutes

Instructions

  1. Open Microsoft PowerPoint (Start Menu, type "PowerPoint")
  2. Click Blank Presentation

  3. Save immediately as "About-Me-Presentation.pptx" on your Desktop (Ctrl+S)

  4. Slide 1 -- Title Slide:

  5. Title: Your full name (font size 40-44)
  6. Subtitle: "A Brief Introduction" (font size 24)

  7. If you have a professional photo of yourself, insert it on this slide

  8. Slide 2 -- Education:

  9. Press Ctrl+M to add a new slide
  10. Choose the "Title and Content" layout
  11. Title: "My Education"
  12. Content: Bullet list of your educational background (school, college, courses)
  13. Maximum 5 bullet points, each 4-6 words

  14. Add a relevant icon (Insert tab, Icons, search "education")

  15. Slide 3 -- Skills:

  16. Title: "My Skills"

  17. Create a visual layout using shapes:

    • Insert 4-6 rounded rectangles (Insert tab, Shapes)
    • Type one skill in each rectangle (e.g., "MS Word," "MS Excel," "Email," "Internet")
    • Color-code them using 2-3 colors
    • Arrange them neatly on the slide

  18. Slide 4 -- Hobbies:

  19. Title: "My Hobbies & Interests"
  20. Use images instead of text -- insert 3-4 images that represent your hobbies
  21. Use free images from pexels.com or Microsoft's stock images

  22. Add short captions below each image if needed

  23. Slide 5 -- Future Goals:

  24. Title: "My Goals"
  25. Write 3 short-term goals and 3 long-term goals

  26. Use two separate text boxes, one labeled "Short-Term" and one "Long-Term"

  27. Apply a consistent design:

  28. Go to the Design tab and choose a theme that you like

  29. Make sure the same theme applies to all slides

  30. Apply a transition:

    • Go to the Transitions tab
    • Choose Fade
    • Set duration to 0.70 seconds
    • Click Apply To All

  31. Save the file (Ctrl+S)

Expected Result

  • A 5-slide presentation with a consistent design theme
  • Each slide has a clear title and visual content (shapes, icons, or images)
  • Text is minimal -- no slide has more than 6 lines of text
  • Fade transition is applied to all slides
  • Saved as "About-Me-Presentation.pptx"

Bonus Challenge

Add Speaker Notes to each slide. Write 2-3 sentences per slide about what you would say if you were presenting to an audience. Practice presenting with Presenter View (Slide Show tab, check "Use Presenter View," then press F5).

Task 2: Topic Presentation (10 Slides)

Difficulty: Intermediate
Time: 40 minutes

Instructions

Choose any topic you are genuinely interested in. Examples: A favorite sport, a historical event, a technology you find fascinating, a place you want to visit, a career you want to pursue, or a social issue you care about.

  1. Open PowerPoint, create a new blank presentation

  2. Save as "Topic-Presentation.pptx" on your Desktop

  3. Plan your 10 slides before you start designing:

Slide # Purpose Content Type
1 Title Slide Title + your name + date
2 Introduction Why this topic matters (text + image)
3 Key Fact/History Background information (text + image)
4 Main Point 1 Detailed explanation (bullet points)
5 Main Point 2 Detailed explanation (bullet points + icon)
6 Data/Statistics A chart (Insert, Chart)
7 Comparison A table comparing two things
8 Main Point 3 Visual slide (mostly image with minimal text)
9 Summary Key takeaways (3-4 bullet points)
10 Thank You / Q&A "Thank You" + your contact info
  1. Create all 10 slides following the plan above. Make sure to include:
  2. At least 4 images (from your computer or free stock photos)
  3. 1 chart (Insert tab, Chart -- any type appropriate to your data)
  4. 1 table (Insert tab, Table -- for comparison)
  5. Bullet points on at least 3 slides

  6. Speaker Notes on every slide (2-3 sentences about what you would say)

  7. Design rules to follow:

  8. Use ONE design theme throughout (Design tab)
  9. Maximum 2 fonts
  10. Maximum 3 colors (plus white/black)
  11. No text smaller than 18pt

  12. One idea per slide

  13. Apply one transition to all slides (Fade or Push recommended)

  14. Add one animation:

  15. On at least 2 slides, animate the bullet points to appear "By Paragraph"
  16. Use Appear or Fade entrance animation

  17. Do NOT add more than 2 animation types in the whole presentation

  18. Save the file

Expected Result

  • A polished 10-slide presentation on a topic of your choice
  • Includes images, a chart, a table, and bullet points
  • Speaker Notes on every slide
  • Consistent design theme, one transition type, minimal animations
  • Professional-looking and ready to present

Bonus Challenge

Export the presentation as a PDF (File, Save As, change type to PDF). Notice how the PDF looks compared to the .pptx -- animations and transitions do not appear in the PDF, but the layout is preserved.

Task 3: Redesign a Bad Slide

Difficulty: Intermediate
Time: 20 minutes

Instructions

Below is a "bad" slide filled with too much text and poor formatting. Your job is to redesign it into 3 well-designed slides.

The Bad Slide (type this into Slide 1 of a new presentation):

The Impact of Pollution on Urban Areas and What We Can Do About It

Pollution is a serious problem that affects millions of people living 
in cities around the world. There are three main types of pollution 
that impact urban areas: air pollution which is caused by vehicle 
emissions, factory smoke, and burning of waste materials leading to 
respiratory diseases and reduced air quality; water pollution which 
comes from industrial waste dumping, sewage overflow, and plastic 
waste contaminating rivers and lakes that supply drinking water to 
cities; and noise pollution which is generated by traffic, 
construction, loudspeakers, and industrial machinery that leads to 
hearing problems and increased stress levels. To combat these 
issues, individuals can use public transport, reduce plastic usage, 
plant trees, and support environmental regulations. Governments 
should enforce stricter emission standards, invest in waste 
management infrastructure, and promote renewable energy sources.

Now save this as "Slide-Redesign.pptx" and transform it:

Redesigned Slide 1 -- "Pollution in Our Cities":
- Title: "Pollution in Our Cities"
- One sentence: "3 types of pollution affect millions in urban areas"
- A powerful, relevant image (search free stock photos for "city pollution" or "smog")
- Minimal text, maximum visual impact

Redesigned Slide 2 -- "Types of Urban Pollution":
- Title: "Types of Urban Pollution"
- Three sections using shapes or icons:
- Icon/shape for Air Pollution: "Vehicle emissions, factory smoke, waste burning"
- Icon/shape for Water Pollution: "Industrial waste, sewage, plastic contamination"
- Icon/shape for Noise Pollution: "Traffic, construction, loudspeakers"
- Use icons from Insert tab, Icons
- Each type gets 5-8 words only

Redesigned Slide 3 -- "What Can We Do?":
- Title: "What Can We Do?"
- Split into two columns:
- Left column: "Individuals" -- 4 bullet points (use public transport, reduce plastic, plant trees, support regulations)
- Right column: "Government" -- 3 bullet points (enforce standards, invest in waste management, promote renewables)
- Use the "Two Content" slide layout

Expected Result

  • Slide 1 of your file still shows the original bad slide (for comparison)
  • Slides 2-4 show the three redesigned versions
  • The redesigned slides follow all design principles: minimal text, images/icons, one idea per slide, readable fonts
  • The same information is conveyed far more effectively

Bonus Challenge

Delete the original bad slide and add a title slide at the beginning with "Urban Pollution Awareness" as the title. Now you have a clean 4-slide mini-presentation. Add Fade transitions and Speaker Notes.

Task 4: Practice Presenting with Presenter View

Difficulty: Beginner
Time: 15 minutes

Instructions

This task focuses on the act of presenting, not creating slides. Use the presentation from Task 1 or Task 2.

  1. Open your "About-Me-Presentation.pptx" or "Topic-Presentation.pptx"

  2. Make sure Speaker Notes are added to every slide (if not, add them now -- write 2-3 talking points per slide)

  3. Start the Slide Show:

  4. Press F5 to start from the beginning
  5. Navigate through all slides using the right arrow key or space bar

  6. Press Escape to exit

  7. Practice with Presenter View:

  8. Go to Slide Show tab
  9. Check Use Presenter View (even on a single screen)
  10. Press F5 to start the show
  11. If you have one screen: right-click during the show and select "Show Presenter View"

  12. Notice: you can see your notes, the current slide, and the next slide

  13. Practice these navigation tricks during the show:

  14. Press B to make the screen black (press B again to resume) -- useful when you want the audience to focus on you, not the screen
  15. Press W to make the screen white (press W again to resume)
  16. Type a slide number (e.g., 3) and press Enter to jump directly to slide 3

  17. Press Escape to end the show

  18. Timed Practice:

  19. Start the show again (F5)
  20. Present each slide aloud (speak as if you have an audience)
  21. Try to spend about 1-2 minutes per slide

  22. Notice the timer in Presenter View showing how long you have been presenting

  23. Write down:

  24. Total time for your presentation: ___ minutes
  25. Which slide was hardest to talk about? ___
  26. Which slide flowed most naturally? ___

Expected Result

  • You have practiced presenting your slides at least twice
  • You know how to use Presenter View, navigate during a show, and use B/W screen shortcuts
  • You have a sense of timing for your presentation
  • You are more comfortable speaking while advancing slides

Bonus Challenge

Record yourself presenting using your phone's camera or your computer's screen recording feature (Windows 11: press Win+G for the Xbox Game Bar, then click Record). Watch the recording. Notice your speaking pace, eye contact (are you looking at the screen or the audience?), and clarity. This self-review is the fastest way to improve.

Task 5: Real-Life Project -- Professional Presentation

Difficulty: Intermediate to Advanced
Time: 45 minutes

Instructions

Choose ONE of these three scenarios and create a complete presentation:

Option A: Propose a New Product to Your Company
- You work at a company and want to propose a new product idea
- 12-15 slides covering: the problem, your solution, target market, competitive analysis (table), projected revenue (chart), timeline, team needed, budget, and conclusion

Option B: School Project on Climate Change
- A comprehensive educational presentation on climate change
- 12-15 slides covering: what is climate change, causes, effects on environment, effects on humans, data/statistics (chart), comparison of solutions (table), what individuals can do, what governments can do, and call to action

Option C: Pitch a Small Business Idea to Investors
- You have a business idea and need to convince investors to fund it
- 12-15 slides covering: the problem you solve, your solution, how it works, market size, revenue model, competitive advantage (table), financial projections (chart), team, funding needed, and vision

Requirements for ALL options:

  1. 12-15 slides with the structure planned before you start designing
  2. At least 5 images (use free stock photos from Unsplash or Pexels)
  3. 1 chart with real or realistic data
  4. 1 table for comparison or structured information
  5. Shapes and/or icons on at least 2 slides
  6. Speaker Notes on every slide (3-5 talking points each)
  7. Consistent design theme (choose from Design tab)
  8. One transition type applied to all slides
  9. Bullet point animations on at least 3 slides (Appear or Fade, By Paragraph)
  10. Slide Master: Add a small logo or your name in the corner of every slide (View tab, Slide Master, insert text or image on the master slide)
  11. Follow ALL design principles: minimal text, high contrast, 2-3 colors, 2 fonts, white space

After creating the presentation:

  1. Save as .pptx (Ctrl+S)
  2. Export as PDF (File, Save As, PDF)
  3. Practice presenting using Presenter View at least once

Expected Result

  • A professional, 12-15 slide presentation ready for a real audience
  • Includes all required elements: images, chart, table, shapes/icons, notes
  • Consistent design with proper transitions and minimal animations
  • Slide Master used for branding (name or logo on all slides)
  • Exported as both .pptx and .pdf
  • You have practiced presenting it at least once

Bonus Challenge

Export the presentation as a video (File, Export, Create a Video). Choose HD (720p) quality and set 5 seconds per slide. Save the video file and play it in your media player. This creates a self-running version you could share on social media or play on a TV at an event.
