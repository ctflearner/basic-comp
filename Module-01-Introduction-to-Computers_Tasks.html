Module 1: Tasks & Assignments

Practice Task 1: Identify All Parts of Your Computer

Objective: Recognise and name every visible part of your computer or laptop.
Difficulty: Beginner
Time: 20 minutes

Instructions

  1. Get a piece of paper and a pen.
  2. Look at your laptop or desktop computer carefully.
  3. On the paper, draw a simple outline of your computer (it does not need to be artistic -- a rectangle is fine).
  4. Point to each part listed below and write its name on your drawing with an arrow:
  5. Screen (monitor)
  6. Keyboard
  7. Touchpad (on a laptop) or mouse (on a desktop)
  8. Power button
  9. Webcam (small circle at top of screen)
  10. Speaker grilles (small holes, usually on the bottom or sides)
  11. USB ports (rectangular or oval slots on the sides)
  12. Headphone jack (small round hole)
  13. Charging port
  14. For each part, write one sentence about what it does. Example: "Screen -- shows me pictures and text."

Expected Result

You should have a labelled drawing with at least 8 parts identified and described. Keep this drawing -- you will use it as a reference.

Bonus Challenge

Find the HDMI port on your device (if it has one). If you have a desktop, also label the tower, any disc drives, and extra USB ports on the back.

Practice Task 2: List 5 Hardware and 5 Software Items

Objective: Demonstrate that you understand the difference between hardware and software.
Difficulty: Beginner
Time: 15 minutes

Instructions

  1. Open a blank piece of paper or a new text file (if you know how).
  2. Draw a line down the middle. Write "HARDWARE" on the left and "SOFTWARE" on the right.
  3. Look at your computer and think about what you use on it.
  4. Write 5 items under HARDWARE. These must be physical things you can touch.
  5. Examples to get you started: keyboard, mouse...
  6. Write 5 items under SOFTWARE. These must be programs or apps.
  7. Examples to get you started: Google Chrome, Calculator app...
  8. For each item, write ONE sentence explaining what it does.

Expected Result

A list of 5 hardware items and 5 software items, each with a short description. None of the items should be repeated. You should be able to explain to someone else why each item is hardware or software.

Bonus Challenge

Can you list 10 of each? Try to think of items that were not mentioned in the lesson.

Practice Task 3: Connect a USB Pendrive and Headphones

Objective: Practice physically connecting devices to your computer.
Difficulty: Beginner
Time: 15 minutes

Instructions

  1. Get a USB pendrive (also called a flash drive or thumb drive) and a pair of wired headphones or earphones.
  2. Connect the pendrive:
    a. Find a USB-A port on the side of your laptop (rectangular slot).
    b. Look at the pendrive. One side of the metal plug has a seam or line, the other side is smooth. The smooth side usually faces up.
    c. Gently push the pendrive into the port. If it does not go in, flip it over and try again. Never force it.
    d. Wait a few seconds. You should hear a sound or see a notification that says a device was connected.
  3. Connect the headphones:
    a. Find the headphone jack on your laptop (small round hole, usually on the left or right side, often marked with a headphone icon).
    b. Push the headphone plug straight into the hole until it is firmly seated.
    c. Put the headphones on your ears.
  4. Test both:
    a. Open File Explorer (the folder icon on your taskbar) and look for the pendrive listed under "This PC."
    b. Play a video or open any website with sound. The sound should come through your headphones, not the laptop speakers.
  5. Disconnect properly:
    a. For headphones: simply pull the plug out gently.
    b. For the pendrive: click the small arrow in the bottom-right corner of your screen (system tray). Look for a USB icon. Click it and select "Eject." Wait for the "Safe to remove" message, then pull the pendrive out.

Expected Result

Your pendrive appears in File Explorer and you can see its contents (or it shows as empty if new). Sound plays through your headphones. You can safely disconnect both.

Bonus Challenge

If you have a Bluetooth mouse or speaker, try connecting it wirelessly. Go to Settings, then Bluetooth, turn it on, and pair the device.

Practice Task 4: Turn Your Computer ON and OFF Properly

Objective: Practice the correct procedure for starting up and shutting down your computer.
Difficulty: Beginner
Time: 10 minutes

Instructions

  1. If your computer is currently on, shut it down first:
    a. Click the Start button (Windows icon at the bottom centre of the screen).
    b. Click the Power icon (circle with a line at the top).
    c. Click Shut down.
    d. Wait until the screen goes completely dark and all lights are off.
  2. Wait 30 seconds.
  3. Now turn your computer ON:
    a. Press the power button once. Do not hold it.
    b. Watch the screen. You will see a logo appear.
    c. Wait patiently. This can take 30 seconds to 2 minutes.
    d. When the desktop or login screen appears, the computer is ready.
    e. If there is a login screen, type your password or PIN and press Enter.
  4. Once you reach the desktop, open any program (for example, click the Start button and type "Calculator," then click on it).
  5. Now shut down again properly:
    a. Close the Calculator by clicking the X in the top-right corner of the window.
    b. Click the Start button.
    c. Click the Power icon.
    d. Click Shut down.

Expected Result

You successfully turned the computer on, opened a program, closed the program, and shut down properly. No error messages appeared. The computer turned off completely.

Bonus Challenge

Try using Restart instead of Shut Down. Notice that the computer turns off and then turns back on by itself. Restart is useful when the computer is behaving strangely.

Practice Task 5: Create Your "My Computer Spec Sheet"

Objective: Find and write down the technical specifications of your own computer.
Difficulty: Beginner
Time: 25 minutes

Instructions

  1. Turn on your computer and get to the desktop.
  2. Find System Information:
    a. Click the Start button (Windows icon at the bottom of the screen).
    b. Type the word "about" using your keyboard.
    c. Click on "About your PC" when it appears in the search results.
    d. A settings window will open showing your computer's details.
  3. Write down the following information on a piece of paper or in a text file:
  4. Device name: (the name of your computer -- shown at the top)
  5. Processor (CPU): (for example, "Intel Core i5" or "AMD Ryzen 5" -- this is the brain of your computer)
  6. Installed RAM: (for example, "8.00 GB" -- this is the short-term memory)
  7. System type: (for example, "64-bit operating system" -- do not worry about what this means yet)
  8. Windows edition: (for example, "Windows 11 Home")
  9. Find your storage space:
    a. Open File Explorer (click the folder icon on the taskbar).
    b. Click on "This PC" in the left sidebar.
    c. Look at the Local Disk (C:) -- it will show something like "120 GB free of 256 GB."
    d. Write this down: "Storage: [total amount], [free space] available"
  10. Organise your spec sheet like this:
MY COMPUTER SPEC SHEET
======================
Date: [today's date]
Device Name: ________
Processor (CPU): ________
RAM (Memory): ________
Storage: ________ total, ________ free
Windows Version: ________
System Type: ________

Expected Result

You have a completed spec sheet with all 6 fields filled in. You know the basic capabilities of your own computer. This information is useful when buying software, asking for tech support, or comparing your computer with others.

Bonus Challenge

Find out your screen resolution: Right-click on the desktop, then click "Display settings." Look for "Display resolution" (for example, 1920 x 1080). Add this to your spec sheet.

Module Assessment

Answer these questions to check your understanding. Write your answers on paper or in a text file.

  1. In your own words, what is a computer? (2-3 sentences)
  2. What are the four things a computer does in order? (Hint: I-P-S-O)
  3. Name three differences between a laptop and a desktop computer.
  4. What is the CPU? What body part is it compared to?
  5. What is the difference between RAM and a hard drive? Give a simple explanation.
  6. Give 3 examples of hardware and 3 examples of software.
  7. What is an input device? Name two examples.
  8. What is an output device? Name two examples.
  9. Why should you never just unplug your computer to turn it off?
  10. What is a USB port used for? Name two things you can plug into it.

Scoring: Each question is worth 1 point. 8 or more correct = you are ready for Module 2. Below 8 = review the topics you got wrong before moving on.

Real-Life Mini Project: "Teach Someone Else"

Objective: Reinforce your learning by teaching what you know to another person.
Time: 30 minutes

Instructions

  1. Find a friend or family member who will listen to you for 10 minutes.
  2. Using your labelled drawing from Task 1 and your spec sheet from Task 5, explain to them:
  3. What a computer is (use the body analogy)
  4. Point to the hardware on your computer and name each part
  5. Show them one software program (open something like Calculator or Notepad)
  6. Show them how to properly turn the computer off
  7. Ask them to name 2 hardware items and 2 software items to check they understood.

Expected Result

You are able to clearly explain basic computer concepts to another person. If they can answer your questions, you both learned something. Teaching is one of the best ways to solidify your own understanding.
