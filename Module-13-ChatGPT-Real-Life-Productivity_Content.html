Topic 1: What is ChatGPT?

Explanation

ChatGPT is an AI (Artificial Intelligence) assistant made by a company called OpenAI. You can talk to it in plain, everyday language — like chatting with a very knowledgeable friend — and it can help you with almost anything: writing, explaining concepts, brainstorming ideas, solving problems, creating plans, debugging code, translating languages, and much more.

Think of it like this: imagine you had a friend who has read millions of books, articles, and websites, never sleeps, never gets annoyed by your questions, and can write anything you ask in seconds. That is ChatGPT.

What ChatGPT Can Do

  • Write: Emails, essays, stories, code, poetry, resumes, reports
  • Explain: Any concept, at any level (simple to expert)
  • Summarize: Long articles, research papers, meeting notes
  • Translate: Between languages
  • Brainstorm: Ideas for projects, businesses, presentations, gifts
  • Solve: Math problems, logic puzzles, coding challenges
  • Plan: Study schedules, travel itineraries, workout routines, budgets
  • Analyze: Data, arguments, pros and cons

What ChatGPT Is NOT

  • Not a search engine: It does not browse the internet in real-time (the free version). It can be wrong about recent events, statistics, and facts.
  • Not always correct: It can generate incorrect information that sounds completely confident. ALWAYS verify important facts.
  • Not a doctor or lawyer: Use it for general information, never for medical diagnoses or legal advice.
  • Not a replacement for learning: Use it as a TOOL to learn faster, not to avoid learning. If ChatGPT writes your entire essay, you learned nothing.

Real-World Example

A second-year engineering student is struggling to understand "Dijkstra's Algorithm" from their textbook. Instead of spending hours re-reading confusing text, they type:

"Explain Dijkstra's Algorithm like I'm 15 years old. Use a real-life analogy and then show me a simple example with 5 nodes."

In 30 seconds, ChatGPT provides a clear explanation using a map/navigation analogy, a step-by-step example with a diagram described in text, and then the Python code to implement it. The student understands in 10 minutes what took 2 hours from the textbook.

Topic 3: The Art of Prompting — How to Ask ChatGPT Effectively

Explanation

The quality of ChatGPT's response depends almost entirely on the quality of your question (called a "prompt"). A vague question gets a vague answer. A specific question gets a specific, useful answer.

Think of it like ordering food at a restaurant. "Give me food" gets you... something. "Give me a vegetable biryani with raita, medium spice, no onions" gets you exactly what you want.

The 5 Rules of Good Prompting

Rule 1: Be Specific

Bad Prompt Good Prompt
"Write an email" "Write a formal email to my landlord requesting repair of the kitchen sink that has been leaking for 2 days. Tone: polite but firm. Keep it under 150 words."
"Tell me about Python" "Explain Python lists to a complete beginner. Include 3 simple examples and common mistakes to avoid."
"Help with my resume" "Create a resume for a fresher with BTech in Computer Science from Mumbai University. Skills: Python, SQL, Excel. Looking for data analyst roles. Use a clean, professional format."

Rule 2: Give Context

Tell ChatGPT WHO you are and what situation you are in:

"I'm a 2nd year computer science student in India with no work experience.
I need to write a cover letter for a summer internship at TCS.
My skills are: Python, SQL, HTML, and basic data analysis.
Write a cover letter that is professional but shows enthusiasm.
Keep it under 250 words."

Without context, ChatGPT will give you a generic response. With context, it gives you something tailored to YOUR situation.

Rule 3: Specify the Format

Tell ChatGPT HOW you want the answer:

  • "Give me a numbered list of..."
  • "Present this as a table with columns for..."
  • "Explain in 5 bullet points"
  • "Write it as a step-by-step guide"
  • "Format it as a comparison chart"
  • "Give me the answer in under 100 words"

Rule 4: Assign a Role

Tell ChatGPT to act as a specific expert:

  • "Act as a career counselor. I have a BTech in CSE with 0 experience. What entry-level jobs can I apply for?"
  • "Act as a fitness trainer. Create a beginner workout plan for someone with no equipment."
  • "Act as a Python teacher. Explain decorators to a beginner using simple analogies."
  • "Act as an interview coach. Give me tough questions for a software developer role and help me answer them."

Rule 5: Iterate and Refine

Your first prompt rarely gives the perfect answer. That is normal. Build on it:

  1. First prompt: "Write a resume for a data analyst fresher"
  2. Follow-up: "Make the summary section more impactful"
  3. Follow-up: "Add a Projects section with 2 academic projects"
  4. Follow-up: "Make it fit on one page"
  5. Follow-up: "Remove the objective section and add skills in a sidebar format"

Each follow-up builds on the previous response. ChatGPT remembers the entire conversation.

Prompt Template (Use This as a Starting Point)

I am a [who you are].
I need [what you want].
The context is [why/situation].
Please format it as [how you want it].
The tone should be [formal/casual/friendly/professional].
Keep it to [length constraint].

Topic 4: ChatGPT for Students

4.1 Explaining Difficult Concepts

When your textbook is confusing, ask ChatGPT to explain it differently:

Prompt examples:

Explain the concept of "normalization in databases" like I'm 15 years old.
Use a real-life analogy first, then the technical definition.

I'm studying Operating Systems. Explain the difference between
process and thread using a restaurant analogy.
Then give me 3 exam-style questions with answers.

I don't understand recursion in programming. Explain it step by step
with a simple example. Show me what happens in memory at each step.

Key tip: If the first explanation is still confusing, say: "Simpler please" or "Explain like I'm 10 years old" or "Give me a different analogy."

4.2 Summarizing Long Texts

When you have a 20-page article or chapter to study:

  1. Copy the text (or the key sections)
  2. Paste it into ChatGPT
  3. Prompt: "Summarize this in 5 key points. Then give me 3 exam questions based on this."

Prompt:

Summarize the following text in:
1. A one-line summary
2. 5 key bullet points
3. 3 potential exam questions with answers

[paste the text here]

4.3 Practice Questions and Quizzes

Prompt:

Give me 10 multiple-choice questions on "Data Structures - Linked Lists"
at an undergraduate level. Include:
- 4 options per question
- The correct answer
- A brief explanation of WHY it's correct

Prompt:

I have an exam on Indian Constitution - Fundamental Rights tomorrow.
Give me 15 short-answer questions with model answers.
Focus on Articles 14-21.

4.4 Essay and Assignment Help

Important: Use ChatGPT to help you THINK and STRUCTURE, not to write the whole thing for you. That is academic dishonesty, and you will not learn anything.

Good use:

Create an outline for a 1000-word essay on "Impact of Social Media
on Mental Health." Give me:
- 5 main sections with subpoints
- 3 statistics I should look up
- A strong opening line suggestion
- A strong closing line suggestion

Then write the essay yourself, using the outline as a guide.

4.5 Study Schedule Creation

Prompt:

Create a 2-week study schedule for my end-semester exams.

Subjects: Data Structures, DBMS, Computer Networks, Discrete Math, English
Exam dates: DS (Dec 5), DBMS (Dec 7), CN (Dec 9), DM (Dec 11), Eng (Dec 13)
Available hours per day: 6 hours (4 PM to 10 PM)
Weak subjects: DBMS and Computer Networks (need more time)
I need breaks every 90 minutes.

Format as a daily table with time slots.

4.6 Grammar and Writing Improvement

Prompt:

Fix the grammar and improve the clarity of the following paragraph.
Keep the meaning exactly the same. Explain what you changed and why.

[paste your paragraph here]

This is a great way to learn — ChatGPT does not just fix your writing, it explains the corrections so you understand the grammar rules.

Topic 5: ChatGPT for Job Seekers

5.1 Resume Writing

Prompt:

Create a professional resume for a fresher with the following details:

Name: [Your Name]
Education: BTech in Computer Science, [University], Graduated 2026, CGPA 7.8
Skills: Python, SQL, HTML/CSS, Microsoft Excel, Google Sheets

Target role: Junior Data Analyst or Software Developer Trainee

Follow-up prompts to refine:
- "Make the summary more impactful — focus on problem-solving skills"
- "Add a section for extracurricular activities: college coding club member, organized tech fest"
- "Rewrite using stronger action verbs"

5.2 Cover Letter Writing

- Basic understanding of databases
- Good communication skills

Tone: Professional but enthusiastic. Show genuine interest in the company.

5.3 Interview Preparation

Prompt:

Act as an interview coach for IT companies in India.

I'm preparing for a Junior Software Developer interview at Wipro.
Give me:
1. 10 common technical interview questions with sample answers
2. 5 HR/behavioral questions with sample answers
3. 3 questions I should ask the interviewer
4. Common mistakes freshers make in interviews

Focus on Python and SQL since those are my strongest skills.

Mock interview prompt:

Let's do a mock interview. You are the interviewer for a
Junior Data Analyst role at TCS.

Ask me one question at a time. After I answer, give me:
- A score out of 10
- What was good about my answer
- How I could improve it
- Then ask the next question

Start with an easy HR question, then move to technical.

5.4 LinkedIn Profile

Prompt:

Background: BTech CSE student (final year), skills in Python, SQL,
web development. Interested in data analysis and cybersecurity.
Looking for entry-level opportunities.
Achievements: Built a portfolio website, completed 3 online certifications,
active in college coding club.

Tone: Professional but personable.
Length: 150-200 words.
Include a call to action at the end (open to opportunities).

5.5 Salary Negotiation

Prompt:

Act as a career counselor specializing in the Indian IT industry.

I received a job offer for Junior Software Developer in Bangalore.
Offered: Rs. 4.5 LPA

2. What is the typical salary range for this role in Bangalore?

After a meeting, paste the key points or transcript:

[paste your notes]

1. Organize these into clear meeting minutes
2. List all action items with who is responsible
3. Highlight any decisions that were made

Prompt:

Create a weekly project status report template that I can fill in
every Friday. Include sections for:
- Tasks completed this week
- Tasks in progress
- Blockers/challenges
- Plan for next week
- Key metrics (leave blank for me to fill)

Format: Professional, clean, ready to send to my manager.

6.4 Data Analysis Help

Prompt:

I have monthly sales data in Excel for 12 months.
Columns: Month, Product, Units Sold, Revenue, Region

Tell me:
1. What Excel formulas should I use to find trends?
2. How to calculate month-over-month growth rate
3. Which chart types are best for showing this data
4. How to create a pivot table to summarize by region

Give me the exact formulas with cell references (assume data starts at A1).

6.5 Presentation Content

Prompt:

Create an outline for a 10-slide presentation on
"Benefits of Cloud Computing for Small Businesses."

Audience: Non-technical business owners
Include for each slide:
- Slide title
- 3-4 bullet points
- Speaker notes (what to say, in simple language)
- Suggestion for a visual/image

Keep the language simple — avoid technical jargon.

Topic 8: What ChatGPT Gets Wrong — Critical Limitations

Explanation

This is the MOST IMPORTANT section of this module. ChatGPT is powerful, but it has real limitations that you must understand. Using it without knowing these limitations is dangerous.

Limitation 1: It Can Be Confidently Wrong

ChatGPT sometimes generates completely false information and presents it as fact, with full confidence. This is called "hallucination."

Example: Ask ChatGPT for "the best restaurants near Mumbai Central station" — it might list restaurant names that do not exist or give wrong addresses. It is not lying; it is generating plausible-sounding text based on patterns, not looking up real data.

Rule: ALWAYS verify important facts using Google or trusted sources. Never submit AI-generated facts without checking them.

Limitation 2: Not a Search Engine

The free version of ChatGPT does not browse the internet. Its knowledge has a cutoff date. It cannot tell you:
- Today's news
- Current stock prices
- Live scores
- Recent events after its training cutoff

For current information: Use Google, Perplexity AI, or the paid ChatGPT Plus (which has browsing).

Limitation 3: Not a Medical or Legal Advisor

  • "ChatGPT says I might have [disease]" — WRONG approach. See a real doctor.
  • "ChatGPT says this contract clause means..." — WRONG approach. Consult a real lawyer.
  • Use ChatGPT for general health/legal INFORMATION, never for personal medical/legal DECISIONS.

Limitation 4: Privacy Concerns

Do NOT share with ChatGPT:
- Your Aadhaar number, PAN card number
- Bank account details, credit/debit card numbers
- Passwords or PINs
- Confidential company documents
- Private medical records

Anything you type into ChatGPT may be used to train future models (unless you opt out in settings). Treat it like a public conversation.

Limitation 5: It Does Not Replace Learning

If ChatGPT writes your entire essay, you learned nothing. If ChatGPT solves your entire assignment, you gained no skill. The exam hall does not have ChatGPT.

Right way: Use it to understand concepts, get unstuck, check your work, and learn faster.
Wrong way: Copy-paste answers without understanding them.

Limitation 6: Bias

ChatGPT can reflect biases present in its training data. Be aware that its responses might:
- Favor certain viewpoints over others
- Make cultural assumptions
- Present opinions as facts

Always think critically about responses, especially on sensitive or controversial topics.

Topic 9: Other AI Tools Worth Knowing

Google Gemini

  • What: Google's AI assistant (formerly called Bard)
  • Access: gemini.google.com (free with Google account)
  • Strength: Integrated with Google services (Gmail, Docs, Sheets). Can search the internet in real-time.
  • Best for: Questions that need current information, integration with Google Workspace

Microsoft Copilot

  • What: Microsoft's AI assistant, built into Windows 11, Edge, and Microsoft Office
  • Access: Built into Windows 11 (press Windows key + C) and at copilot.microsoft.com
  • Strength: Integrated with Microsoft apps. Can help you write in Word, analyze in Excel, create presentations in PowerPoint.
  • Best for: Users who work primarily with Microsoft tools

Perplexity AI

  • What: An AI-powered search engine with citations
  • Access: perplexity.ai (free)
  • Strength: Every answer includes links to sources so you can verify information. Combines AI + search.
  • Best for: Research and fact-finding where you need citations and sources

Canva AI

  • What: AI-powered design tools inside Canva (the design platform)
  • Access: canva.com (free tier available)
  • Strength: Generate social media posts, presentations, logos, and designs with AI
  • Best for: Visual design, social media content, presentations
