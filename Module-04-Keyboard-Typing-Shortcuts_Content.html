Topic 1: Keyboard Layout

Explanation

The keyboard is divided into sections. Understanding each section helps you find keys quickly without looking down.

The QWERTY Layout

The letter keys are arranged in a pattern called QWERTY (named after the first six letters in the top-left row: Q-W-E-R-T-Y). This layout was designed in the 1870s for typewriters and has been used ever since.

The letter keys are arranged in three rows:
- Top row: Q W E R T Y U I O P
- Middle row (Home row): A S D F G H J K L ;
- Bottom row: Z X C V B N M , . /

The middle row is called the Home row because this is where your fingers rest when not typing. We will cover this more in the touch typing section.

Function Keys (F1 through F12)

These are the keys along the very top of the keyboard, labelled F1, F2, F3, all the way to F12.

Key Common Use
F1 Opens Help in most programs
F2 Renames a selected file or folder
F3 Opens the search bar in File Explorer
F4 (With Alt) Closes the current window
F5 Refreshes a web page or File Explorer
F11 Toggles full-screen mode in browsers
F12 Opens "Save As" in Microsoft Office apps

On laptops, the function keys often double as media keys (volume, brightness, WiFi). You may need to hold the Fn key (bottom-left of the keyboard) while pressing a function key to use its F-number function.

Modifier Keys

These keys do nothing on their own. You hold them down while pressing another key.

Key Name What It Does
Ctrl Control Combined with other keys for shortcuts (Ctrl+C = copy)
Alt Alternate Used for menu shortcuts and Alt+Tab
Shift Shift Makes letters uppercase; accesses the top symbol on a key
Win Windows key Opens Start Menu; used for many Windows shortcuts
Fn Function On laptops, accesses secondary functions of keys

Special Keys

Key What It Does
Enter Confirms an action, starts a new line when typing
Backspace Deletes the character to the LEFT of the cursor
Delete Deletes the character to the RIGHT of the cursor (or deletes a selected file)
Tab Moves to the next field in a form; adds indentation when typing
Caps Lock Locks all letters to UPPERCASE. Press again to unlock. A light usually turns on when active.
Space bar Adds a space between words (the long bar at the bottom)
Esc (Escape) Cancels the current action, closes menus and pop-ups
Print Screen (PrtSc) Takes a screenshot of your entire screen
Home Jumps to the beginning of a line
End Jumps to the end of a line
Page Up / Page Down Scrolls up or down one full page in a document

Number Pad (Numpad)

Full-sized desktop keyboards have a number pad on the right side -- a block of number keys arranged like a calculator. This is very useful for typing numbers quickly.

  • Num Lock must be ON for the numpad to type numbers (there is a Num Lock key and usually a small light indicator).
  • If Num Lock is OFF, the numpad keys act as navigation keys (arrows, Home, End).
  • Most laptops do NOT have a numpad due to their smaller size.

Key Points to Remember

  • QWERTY is the standard letter layout
  • Modifier keys (Ctrl, Alt, Shift, Win) are used in combination with other keys
  • Function keys (F1-F12) have special purposes in different programs
  • Backspace deletes left; Delete deletes right
  • Caps Lock makes everything uppercase -- check the light before typing

Topic 2: Mouse and Touchpad

Explanation

The mouse (or touchpad on a laptop) is how you point at things on the screen and interact with them.

Mouse Actions:

Action How to Do It What It Does
Left-click Press the left button once Select an item, press a button
Double-click Press the left button twice quickly Open a file, folder, or app
Right-click Press the right button once Open a menu with options (copy, paste, rename, delete, etc.)
Scroll Roll the wheel between the buttons up or down Scroll through a page or document
Middle-click Press down on the scroll wheel In a browser: opens a link in a new tab
Click-and-drag Hold the left button down while moving the mouse Move a file from one place to another, or select text

Touchpad Actions (for laptops):

The touchpad is the flat rectangular area below your keyboard. You use your finger on it instead of a separate mouse.

Action How to Do It Same As
Move cursor Slide one finger across the touchpad Moving the mouse
Left-click Tap the touchpad once with one finger (or press the bottom-left area) Left mouse button
Right-click Tap the touchpad with two fingers at the same time Right mouse button
Scroll Slide two fingers up or down on the touchpad Mouse scroll wheel
Zoom in/out Pinch two fingers together or spread them apart Ctrl plus scroll wheel
Switch apps Swipe three fingers left or right Alt+Tab
Show Desktop Swipe three fingers downward Win+D

Step-by-Step Guide: Practising Mouse/Touchpad Skills

  1. Left-click practice: Open File Explorer. Click on different folders in the left sidebar. Notice how each click selects a different folder.
  2. Double-click practice: Go to your Desktop. Double-click on any icon to open it. If it opens, you did it correctly. Close it with the X button.
  3. Right-click practice: Right-click on an empty area of the desktop. A menu appears with options like "Personalise," "Display settings," etc. Click anywhere else to close the menu.
  4. Scroll practice: Open a web page with lots of text. Scroll up and down using the scroll wheel (mouse) or two-finger scroll (touchpad).
  5. Drag practice: On the desktop, left-click on an icon and hold the button down. While holding, move the mouse. The icon moves with you. Release to drop it in the new location.

Key Points to Remember

  • Left-click selects, double-click opens
  • Right-click always shows a menu with options
  • Two-finger tap on a touchpad = right-click
  • Two-finger scroll on a touchpad = scrolling up and down
  • Drag = hold the left button and move

Topic 3: On-Screen Keyboard

Explanation

The on-screen keyboard is a virtual keyboard that appears on your screen. You click the keys with your mouse or tap them on a touchscreen.

When it is useful:
- Your physical keyboard has a broken key
- You are using a tablet or touchscreen device
- You need to type special characters not on your physical keyboard

Step-by-Step Guide: Enabling the On-Screen Keyboard

  1. Click the Start button.
  2. Type "On-screen keyboard" and click on it. Or go to Settings, then Accessibility, then Keyboard, and turn on "On-screen keyboard."
  3. A keyboard appears on your screen.
  4. Click the keys with your mouse to type.
  5. To close it, click the X on the on-screen keyboard.

Key Points to Remember

  • The on-screen keyboard is an accessibility tool
  • Useful when a physical key is broken or on touchscreen devices
  • Find it through Start Menu search or Settings, Accessibility, Keyboard

Topic 4: Essential Windows Shortcuts

Explanation

Keyboard shortcuts let you do common tasks much faster than using the mouse. Instead of clicking through menus, you press two or three keys at the same time.

Learning shortcuts feels slow at first, but once they become habit, they save you enormous amounts of time. Professional workers use shortcuts constantly.

How to use a shortcut: Hold down the first key(s), then press the last key. For example, "Ctrl+C" means: hold Ctrl, press C, then release both.

The Most Important Shortcuts

Editing Shortcuts (use in any program):

Shortcut What It Does Easy Way to Remember
Ctrl+C Copy selected text or file C for Copy
Ctrl+V Paste what you copied V is next to C on the keyboard
Ctrl+X Cut (copy and remove the original) X looks like scissors
Ctrl+Z Undo the last action (go back one step) Z for "reverse"
Ctrl+Y Redo (undo the undo) Y for "Yes, do it again"
Ctrl+A Select All text or files A for All
Ctrl+S Save the current document S for Save -- THE MOST IMPORTANT SHORTCUT
Ctrl+P Print the current document P for Print
Ctrl+F Find text in a document or web page F for Find

Ctrl+S deserves special attention. Save your work constantly. Every few minutes. Every time you finish a paragraph. Before you stand up. Before you switch to another app. If your computer crashes and you have not saved, your work is gone.

Windows Navigation Shortcuts:

Shortcut What It Does
Alt+Tab Switch between open apps (hold Alt, tap Tab repeatedly to cycle)
Win+D Show the Desktop (minimise all windows). Press again to restore.
Win+E Open File Explorer
Win+L Lock your computer (shows the login screen without closing apps)
Win+Shift+S Take a screenshot of a selected area
PrtSc (Print Screen) Capture the entire screen to clipboard
Alt+F4 Close the current app or window
Ctrl+Shift+Esc Open Task Manager

Win+L is critical for security. When you leave your desk at work, at a library, or at a cafe, ALWAYS press Win+L to lock your computer. This prevents anyone from accessing your files while you are away. When you return, just type your password to unlock.

Step-by-Step Guide: Practising Copy, Cut, and Paste

  1. Open Notepad (Start, type "Notepad," click it).
  2. Type this sentence: "I am learning keyboard shortcuts today."
  3. Now select all the text: Press Ctrl+A. The entire sentence turns blue (highlighted).
  4. Copy it: Press Ctrl+C. Nothing visible happens, but the text is now in the clipboard (a temporary holding area).
  5. Press Enter to go to a new line.
  6. Paste it: Press Ctrl+V. The same sentence appears again on the new line.
  7. Select the second copy of the sentence (click at the start, hold Shift, click at the end).
  8. Cut it: Press Ctrl+X. The selected text disappears (it is now in the clipboard).
  9. Press Enter to go to a new line.
  10. Paste it: Press Ctrl+V. The text appears on the new line.
  11. Undo: Press Ctrl+Z. The last paste is undone. Press Ctrl+Z again to undo more steps.
  12. Redo: Press Ctrl+Y to redo what you just undid.
  13. Save: Press Ctrl+S. A "Save As" dialog appears (first time only). Choose a location and filename, then click Save.

Step-by-Step Guide: Taking Screenshots

Method 1: Win+Shift+S (Recommended -- select an area)
1. Press Win+Shift+S. The screen dims.
2. Click and drag to select the area you want to capture.
3. The screenshot is copied to your clipboard.
4. Open Paint, Word, or an email and press Ctrl+V to paste it.
5. Or: a notification appears in the bottom-right. Click it to open Snipping Tool where you can save the screenshot as a file.

Method 2: PrtSc (Capture the entire screen)
1. Press the PrtSc (Print Screen) key. The entire screen is copied to your clipboard.
2. Open Paint or any program and press Ctrl+V to paste it.

Key Points to Remember

  • Ctrl+S is the most important shortcut -- save your work constantly
  • Ctrl+C, Ctrl+V, Ctrl+X (copy, paste, cut) are used dozens of times a day
  • Win+L locks your computer -- use it every time you leave your desk
  • Alt+Tab switches between open apps quickly
  • Win+Shift+S takes a screenshot of a selected area

Topic 5: Touch Typing Introduction

Explanation

Touch typing means typing without looking at the keyboard. Your fingers know where each key is by feel and muscle memory, like how a pianist plays without looking at each key.

Why it matters:
- Speed: Touch typists type 40-80 words per minute (WPM). Hunt-and-peck typists manage 10-30 WPM.
- Accuracy: Looking at the screen while typing means you catch errors immediately.
- Productivity: In any job that involves a computer, faster typing means more work done in less time.
- Comfort: You do not strain your neck by looking down at the keyboard constantly.

The Home Row

The home row is the middle row of letter keys: A S D F G H J K L ;

This is where your fingers rest when not actively typing a key. Your fingers always return here after reaching for other keys.

Finger placement:

Left Hand Key
Left pinky A
Left ring finger S
Left middle finger D
Left index finger F (and G -- your index finger reaches over)
Right Hand Key
Right index finger J (and H -- your index finger reaches over)
Right ring finger L
Right middle finger K
Right pinky ;

Both thumbs rest on the Space bar.

How to find the home row without looking: Feel the keys F and J. They have a small bump or ridge on them. These bumps help your index fingers find the home row by touch.

Step-by-Step Guide: Placing Your Fingers on the Home Row

  1. Sit up straight with your feet flat on the floor.
  2. Place your fingers on the keyboard.
  3. Feel for the bumps on the F and J keys with your index fingers.
  4. Place your left hand fingers on A (pinky), S (ring), D (middle), F (index).
  5. Place your right hand fingers on J (index), K (middle), L (ring), ; (pinky).
  6. Rest both thumbs lightly on the Space bar.
  7. This is your starting position. Always return to this position after typing any key.

How each finger reaches other keys:

Each finger is responsible for the keys directly above and below its home position:
- Left pinky: A, Q, Z (and Shift, Tab, Caps Lock)
- Left ring: S, W, X
- Left middle: D, E, C
- Left index: F, G, R, T, V, B
- Right index: J, H, U, Y, N, M
- Right middle: K, I, comma
- Right ring: L, O, period
- Right pinky: semicolon, P, forward slash (and Enter, Backspace, Shift)

Key Points to Remember

  • Home row: A S D F G H J K L ;
  • F and J keys have bumps to help you find home row by touch
  • Each finger has assigned keys above and below its home position
  • Always return to the home row after pressing a key
  • Touch typing takes weeks of practice but is worth the effort

Topic 6: Typing Practice Tools and Daily Routine

Explanation

Learning to touch type requires daily practice. The following free websites are excellent for beginners.

Recommended Typing Practice Websites:

Website Best For Cost
TypingClub (typingclub.com) Structured lessons from scratch, great for beginners Free
Keybr.com Adapts to your weak keys, focuses on what you need to practice Free
MonkeyType (monkeytype.com) Speed tests and tracking progress Free

Daily Typing Routine (15 minutes per day):

Week Focus Practice
Weeks 1-2 Home row only (A S D F G H J K L ;) TypingClub lessons 1-10
Weeks 3-4 Add top row (Q W E R T Y U I O P) TypingClub lessons 11-20
Weeks 5-6 Add bottom row (Z X C V B N M) TypingClub lessons 21-30
Weeks 7-8 Numbers and common punctuation Keybr.com for targeted practice
Ongoing Speed building MonkeyType 1-minute tests

Tracking your progress:
- Record your WPM (words per minute) at the end of each week.
- Beginners typically start at 10-15 WPM.
- After 2 months of daily practice, most people reach 30-40 WPM.
- After 6 months, 50-60 WPM is achievable.
- Accuracy is more important than speed. Aim for 95% accuracy before trying to go faster.

Real-World Example

At a typical office job, you might type emails, reports, and messages for 2-4 hours a day. A person typing at 20 WPM takes an hour to type a 1,200-word email. A person typing at 60 WPM finishes the same email in 20 minutes. Over a year, that difference adds up to hundreds of hours saved.

Key Points to Remember

  • Practice 15 minutes every day -- consistency is more important than long sessions
  • Start with the home row and add one row at a time
  • Focus on accuracy first, speed comes naturally
  • Track your WPM weekly to see progress
  • TypingClub is the best starting point for complete beginners

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  1. Looking at the keyboard while typing. This slows you down and creates bad habits. Force yourself to look at the screen, even if you make mistakes at first.
  2. Using one or two fingers to type (hunt and peck). Retrain yourself with proper finger placement now. It feels slower initially but pays off enormously.
  3. Forgetting Ctrl+S. Make saving a reflex. Every few minutes. Before switching apps. Before standing up. Before doing anything else.
  4. Not locking your computer when leaving your desk. Win+L takes half a second and protects all your work and accounts.
  5. Pressing Caps Lock instead of Shift for one capital letter. Use Shift for single capital letters (hold Shift, press the letter). Only use Caps Lock when you need MANY uppercase letters.
  6. Ignoring keyboard shortcuts. It feels easier to use the mouse at first, but shortcuts are faster once learned. Force yourself to use them until they become automatic.
  7. Practising typing for hours once a week instead of 15 minutes daily. Short, consistent practice builds muscle memory faster than occasional long sessions.
