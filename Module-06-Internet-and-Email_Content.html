Topic 1: What Is the Internet?

Explanation

Think of the Internet as the world's largest library, post office, and marketplace -- all connected by invisible cables. Just like a library holds books on every topic, the Internet holds information on everything. Just like a post office lets you send and receive letters, the Internet lets you send and receive messages (email, chat). Just like a marketplace lets you buy and sell, the Internet lets you shop online.

How it works (simplified):

Your computer connects to a router (the small box with blinking lights in your home). The router connects to your ISP (Internet Service Provider -- the company you pay for internet, like Jio, Airtel, BSNL, or Comcast). The ISP connects you to the Internet -- the global network of billions of connected computers.

Your Computer --> Router --> ISP --> The Internet (billions of computers worldwide)

Every website you visit is stored on a computer somewhere in the world called a server. When you type a website address, your computer asks that server to send the webpage to you. This happens in less than a second.

Real-World Example

When you open YouTube to watch a video, your computer sends a request through your router, through your ISP, across the Internet to YouTube's servers (which are giant buildings full of computers owned by Google). Those servers send the video back to your computer through the same path. All of this happens in milliseconds.

Key Points

  • The Internet is a global network connecting billions of devices
  • You need an ISP (Internet Service Provider) to access the Internet
  • Websites live on special computers called servers
  • Your path to the Internet: Computer --> Router --> ISP --> Internet
  • WiFi is just a wireless way to connect your device to the router -- WiFi is NOT the Internet itself

Topic 2: Web Browsers

Explanation

A web browser is your window to the Internet. Without a browser, you cannot view websites. It is a program (application) that reads website code and displays it as the pages you see.

Common browsers:

Browser Icon Made By Notes
Google Chrome Round circle with red, yellow, green, and blue Google Most popular, works well with Google services
Microsoft Edge Blue wave shape Microsoft Comes pre-installed on Windows 11
Mozilla Firefox Orange fox wrapping around a globe Mozilla Good for privacy

All browsers do the same basic job. For this course, we will use Google Chrome because it is the most widely used.

Step-by-Step Guide: Opening and Using Chrome

Step 1: Open Google Chrome
- Click the Start button (bottom-left of screen)
- Type Chrome on your keyboard
- Click Google Chrome when it appears in the search results
- Chrome opens with a new tab

Step 2: Understand the Chrome Window

At the very top of Chrome, you will see:

  • Address Bar (URL bar): The long box at the top where you type website addresses. This is also where you can type search queries.
  • Back Button (left arrow): Go back to the previous page
  • Forward Button (right arrow): Go forward (after going back)
  • Refresh Button (circular arrow): Reload the current page
  • Tabs: Each open webpage appears as a tab at the top of the window

Step 3: Navigate to a Website
- Click once on the Address Bar (it will highlight the text inside)
- Type a website address, for example: www.google.com
- Press Enter
- The website loads in the current tab

Step 4: Work with Tabs

Tabs let you have multiple websites open at once, like having multiple books open on your desk.

Action Keyboard Shortcut Mouse Method
Open a new tab Ctrl + T Click the + icon next to the last tab
Close current tab Ctrl + W Click the X on the tab
Switch between tabs Ctrl + Tab Click on the tab you want
Reopen a closed tab Ctrl + Shift + T --

Step 5: Bookmark a Website

A bookmark saves a website so you can find it again easily, like putting a bookmark in a book.

  • Navigate to a website you want to save
  • Press Ctrl + D (or click the star icon in the address bar)
  • A small window appears -- you can change the name if you want
  • Click Done
  • To see your bookmarks: press Ctrl + Shift + B to show the bookmarks bar

Key Points

  • A browser is the program you use to view websites
  • Chrome, Edge, and Firefox are the most common browsers
  • The Address Bar is where you type website addresses
  • Use tabs to have multiple websites open at once
  • Bookmark important websites so you can find them quickly

Topic 3: Understanding URLs

Explanation

A URL (Uniform Resource Locator) is the address of a webpage, just like your home has a street address. Every webpage on the Internet has a unique URL.

Let us break down a URL:

https://www.example.com/about
Part Name What It Means
https:// Protocol The rules for how data is sent. HTTPS means it is secure (encrypted).
www Subdomain A section of the website. "www" stands for World Wide Web.
example Domain name The name of the website (like a person's name).
.com Extension The type of website (.com = commercial, .org = organization, .gov = government, .edu = education, .in = India).
/about Path The specific page on the website (like a room in a building).

Why HTTPS Matters

Look at the address bar when you visit a website. You should see a padlock icon before the URL. This means:

  • Padlock visible (HTTPS): The connection is secure. Data you send (passwords, credit card numbers) is encrypted -- others cannot read it.
  • No padlock or "Not Secure" warning (HTTP): The connection is NOT secure. Do NOT enter passwords or personal information on these sites.

Rule: Never enter passwords or payment information on a website that does not show the padlock icon.

Real-World Example

When you do online banking, you will notice the URL starts with https:// and shows a padlock. This encryption prevents anyone between you and the bank (like someone on the same WiFi) from reading your password or account details.

Key Points

  • A URL is a website's address
  • HTTPS (with padlock) means the site is secure -- safe to enter personal info
  • HTTP (without padlock) means NOT secure -- never enter passwords
  • Common extensions: .com (commercial), .org (organization), .gov (government), .in (India)

Topic 4: Searching Google Effectively

Explanation

Google is the most popular search engine -- a tool that helps you find information on the Internet. Most people just type a few words into Google, but there are techniques to get much better results.

Step-by-Step Guide: Search Techniques

Basic Search:
- Open Google (www.google.com)
- Type what you are looking for in the search box
- Press Enter

Technique 1: Use Specific Keywords

Instead of vague searches, be specific.

Bad Search Better Search
food how to make butter chicken recipe
computer problem Windows 11 laptop not connecting to WiFi
job data entry jobs in Delhi for freshers

Technique 2: Use Quotes for Exact Phrases

Put words in double quotes to search for that exact phrase.

  • Searching: "to be or not to be" -- finds pages with that exact phrase
  • Searching: "error code 0x80070005" -- finds pages about that specific error

Technique 3: Use Minus (-) to Exclude Words

Put a minus sign before a word to exclude it from results.

  • apple -fruit -- searches for Apple (the company), excluding results about the fruit
  • jaguar -car -- searches for the animal, excluding the car brand

Technique 4: Use filetype: to Find Specific Files

  • budget template filetype:xlsx -- finds Excel files with budget templates
  • resume format filetype:pdf -- finds PDF resume templates
  • Python tutorial filetype:pdf -- finds Python tutorials in PDF format

Technique 5: Use Google Images
- Go to Google.com
- Click Images (top right)
- Type your search -- you will see image results
- Useful for: finding pictures, identifying objects, visual references

Technique 6: Use Google Maps
- Go to maps.google.com
- Type a location or address
- You can: get directions, see street view, find nearby restaurants/shops/hospitals

Real-World Example

You need to find a free PDF textbook on basic accounting. Instead of searching "accounting book," you search: basic accounting textbook filetype:pdf free. This gives you direct links to PDF files on accounting.

Key Points

  • Be specific with your search keywords
  • Use "quotes" for exact phrases
  • Use minus (-) to exclude unwanted results
  • Use filetype: to find specific document types
  • Google Images and Google Maps are powerful tools

Topic 5: Internet Safety

Explanation

The Internet is incredibly useful, but it also has dangers. Just like you lock your house door and look both ways before crossing the road, you need to follow safety rules online.

Step-by-Step Guide: Staying Safe Online

Rule 1: Do Not Click Suspicious Links

If you receive a message (email, WhatsApp, SMS) with a link you did not expect, do NOT click it. Signs of suspicious links:
- "You have won a prize! Click here!" -- you did not enter any contest
- "Your account will be closed! Click here immediately!" -- creates false urgency
- Links from unknown senders
- Shortened links (bit.ly/xyz) from unknown sources

Rule 2: Check the URL Before Entering Passwords

Before typing your password on any website:
1. Look at the address bar
2. Check that the URL is correct (for example, google.com not go0gle.com or google.security-check.com)
3. Make sure you see the padlock icon (HTTPS)

Fake website examples:

Real URL Fake URL (DO NOT VISIT)
google.com go0gle.com (zero instead of 'o')
facebook.com faceb00k.com
sbi.co.in sbi-secure-login.com

Rule 3: Handle Pop-up Ads Safely

Pop-up ads are small windows that suddenly appear on your screen.
- NEVER click on pop-up ads, even to close them using their own "X" button
- Instead, press Alt + F4 to close the window, or close the browser tab
- If a pop-up says "Your computer has a virus!" -- it is lying. Close it immediately.
- Install an ad blocker extension (like uBlock Origin) in Chrome for extra protection

Rule 4: Public WiFi Dangers

Public WiFi (in cafes, airports, malls) is convenient but risky. Others on the same network can potentially see your data.

  • NEVER do online banking on public WiFi
  • NEVER enter passwords for important accounts on public WiFi
  • DO use public WiFi for general browsing (reading news, watching videos)
  • For better safety on public WiFi, use a VPN (Virtual Private Network) -- we will cover this in a later module

Rule 5: Create Strong Passwords

  • Minimum 12 characters
  • Mix of: uppercase (A-Z), lowercase (a-z), numbers (0-9), symbols (!@#$%^&*)
  • DO NOT use: your name, birthday, "password123", "123456"
  • Use a different password for each important account
  • Example of a strong password: Mango$River42!Blue

Real-World Example

You receive an email that says: "Dear Customer, your SBI account has been locked. Click here to verify your identity." The email looks official with the SBI logo. But you check the sender's email address -- it is from "sbi.alerts@gmail.com" instead of an official @sbi.co.in address. The link goes to "sbi-verify.fakesite.com." This is a phishing attack -- a fake email trying to steal your password. You delete it.

Key Points

  • Never click links in unexpected messages
  • Always check the URL before entering passwords
  • Close pop-ups using Alt+F4, never click inside them
  • Do not do banking or enter passwords on public WiFi
  • Use strong, unique passwords for each account

Topic 6: Creating a Gmail Account

Explanation

Gmail is Google's free email service. It is one of the most popular email services in the world. Having a Gmail account also gives you access to Google Drive, Google Docs, YouTube, and many other Google services.

Step-by-Step Guide

Step 1: Go to Gmail
- Open Chrome
- Type gmail.com in the address bar
- Press Enter
- Click Create account
- Select For my personal use

Step 2: Fill in Your Information
- First name: Your real first name
- Last name: Your real last name
- Click Next

Step 3: Enter Your Birthday and Gender
- Select your birth month, day, and year
- Select your gender (or prefer not to say)
- Click Next

Step 4: Choose Your Email Address

This is important. Your email address is your professional identity online.

Good Email (Professional) Bad Email (Unprofessional)
priya.sharma@gmail.com coolgirl2005@gmail.com
rahul.verma.dev@gmail.com killerbeast99@gmail.com
firstname.lastname@gmail.com ilovepizza@gmail.com

Tips for choosing your email:
- Use your real name: firstname.lastname or firstnamelastname
- If taken, add a number: priya.sharma42
- Avoid nicknames, jokes, or random words
- This email will go on your resume and job applications

Step 5: Create a Strong Password
- Minimum 12 characters
- Mix uppercase, lowercase, numbers, and symbols
- Example: MyGmail$2024!Secure
- Type it again to confirm
- Write this password down and keep it in a safe place (physically, not on the computer)

Step 6: Add Recovery Information
- Add a phone number (for password recovery and security)
- Add a recovery email (optional, but recommended)
- These help you get back into your account if you forget your password

Step 7: Accept Terms and Finish
- Read (or at least skim) Google's Terms of Service
- Click I agree
- Your Gmail account is now created

Key Points

  • Choose a professional email address using your real name
  • Use a strong password (12+ characters with mixed types)
  • Add a phone number for account recovery
  • Your Gmail account gives you access to all Google services

Topic 8: Composing and Sending an Email

Explanation

Sending an email is like writing a letter, but it arrives instantly. Understanding each part of an email ensures your message is clear and professional.

Step-by-Step Guide

Step 1: Start a New Email
- Click the Compose button (top-left of Gmail, usually a large button with a "+" or pencil icon)
- A new email window appears at the bottom-right of your screen

Step 2: Fill in the Recipients

Field What It Means When to Use
To The main recipient(s) Always -- this is who the email is for
CC (Carbon Copy) Others who should see the email When you want someone informed but they do not need to act
BCC (Blind Carbon Copy) Others who receive a copy, but no one else can see their name When sending to many people who do not know each other (like a newsletter)

Example:
- You are emailing your teacher about a project. You put your teacher in To. You put your project partner in CC (so they know what you said). You put your parent in BCC (they get a copy, but your teacher does not see their email).

Step 3: Write the Subject Line
- The subject line is a brief description of what the email is about
- Keep it clear and specific

Bad Subject Good Subject
Hi Question about Assignment 3 Deadline
Help Request for Leave on 15th November
(blank) Meeting Rescheduled to 3 PM Friday

Step 4: Write the Body

A professional email body has:

  1. Greeting: "Dear Mr./Ms. [Name]," or "Hello [Name],"
  2. Purpose: State why you are writing in the first line
  3. Details: Provide necessary information
  4. Closing request: What you need from them
  5. Sign-off: "Thank you," or "Best regards," followed by your name

Step 5: Format Your Text (Optional)
- At the bottom of the compose window, you will see formatting options:
- B = Bold, I = Italic, U = Underline
- Text color, bullet points, numbered lists
- Keep formatting minimal in professional emails

Step 6: Review and Send
- Read your email once more for spelling and grammar errors
- Check that you have the right recipient in the "To" field
- Click the Send button (blue button with an arrow)

Real-World Example

To: hr.manager@company.com
CC: recruitment@company.com
Subject: Application for Data Entry Position - Priya Sharma

Dear Hiring Manager,

I am writing to express my interest in the Data Entry Position advertised 
on your website. I have completed my BCA and have strong typing skills 
(45 WPM) and proficiency in MS Office.

I have attached my resume for your review. I am available for an interview 
at your convenience.

Thank you for considering my application.

Best regards,
Priya Sharma
Phone: 98765-43210

Key Points

  • Always write a clear, specific subject line
  • Use To for the main recipient, CC to keep others informed, BCC for hidden recipients
  • Follow the professional email structure: greeting, purpose, details, sign-off
  • Always review your email before clicking Send

Topic 9: Replying, Forwarding, and Reply All

Explanation

When you receive an email, you have three options to respond:

Action What It Does When to Use
Reply Sends a response to the person who sent you the email When only the sender needs your response
Reply All Sends a response to the sender AND everyone in CC When everyone in the conversation needs to see your response
Forward Sends the email to someone new who was not in the original conversation When you want to share the email with someone else

Step-by-Step Guide

To Reply:
1. Open the email you want to reply to
2. Click the Reply button (curved arrow icon at the bottom of the email, or top right)
3. Type your response
4. Click Send

To Reply All:
1. Open the email
2. Click the small dropdown arrow next to the Reply button
3. Select Reply All
4. Type your response (remember: EVERYONE on the email will see this)
5. Click Send

To Forward:
1. Open the email
2. Click the Forward button (arrow pointing right)
3. Type the email address of the person you want to send it to
4. Optionally add a note above the forwarded content: "Hi Rahul, please see the email below."
5. Click Send

WARNING about Reply All:
Be very careful with Reply All. If someone sends an email to 50 people and you click Reply All, all 50 people receive your response. This has caused many embarrassing situations in workplaces. Only use Reply All when everyone genuinely needs to see your response.

Key Points

  • Reply goes to the sender only
  • Reply All goes to everyone on the email -- use with caution
  • Forward sends the email to someone new
  • Always double-check who will receive your message before sending

Topic 10: Email Attachments

Explanation

An attachment is a file you send along with an email -- like putting a document inside an envelope with your letter. You can attach documents, photos, PDFs, spreadsheets, and more.

Step-by-Step Guide: Sending Attachments

Step 1: Compose a New Email (or Reply to one)

Step 2: Click the Attachment Icon
- At the bottom of the compose window, look for the paperclip icon
- Click it

Step 3: Select Your File
- A file browser window opens
- Navigate to the file you want to attach
- Click the file, then click Open
- The file appears at the bottom of your email (you will see its name and size)

Step 4: Send the Email
- Click Send as normal

Important Rules for Attachments:

Rule Details
Size limit Gmail allows attachments up to 25 MB. For larger files, use Google Drive (covered below).
Common file types .pdf, .docx, .xlsx, .pptx, .jpg, .png, .zip
Blocked file types Gmail blocks .exe, .bat, .cmd files (because they could contain viruses)

Step-by-Step Guide: Downloading Attachments Safely

Step 1: Open the email that has an attachment (you will see a paperclip icon or file preview at the bottom of the email)

Step 2: Hover over the attachment to see options:
- Download icon (down arrow): Save the file to your Downloads folder
- Google Drive icon: Save directly to your Google Drive

Step 3: Safety Check Before Downloading
- Do you know the sender? If not, do NOT download the attachment.
- Does the file type make sense? If someone sends you a ".exe" file claiming it is a "photo," it is likely a virus.
- Gmail automatically scans attachments for viruses, but always be cautious.

Key Points

  • Use the paperclip icon to attach files
  • Gmail limit is 25 MB per email
  • Never download attachments from unknown senders
  • Gmail blocks dangerous file types (.exe, .bat) for your protection

Topic 11: Email Etiquette for Professional Use

Explanation

Email etiquette is the set of rules for writing polite, clear, and professional emails. Following these rules makes you look competent and respectful.

Professional Email Rules:

  1. Always use a clear subject line -- never leave it blank
  2. Start with a greeting -- "Dear [Name]," or "Hello [Name],"
  3. Get to the point quickly -- state your purpose in the first sentence
  4. Keep it concise -- short paragraphs, no unnecessary details
  5. Use proper grammar and spelling -- do not use SMS language (u, ur, plz, thx)
  6. End with a sign-off -- "Best regards," "Thank you," "Sincerely,"
  7. Add an email signature -- your name, phone number, designation

Setting Up an Email Signature in Gmail:
1. Click the gear icon (Settings) in the top right
2. Click See all settings
3. Scroll down to Signature
4. Click Create new
5. Type your signature. Example:

Best regards,
Priya Sharma
Phone: 98765-43210
Email: priya.sharma@gmail.com
  1. Click Save Changes at the bottom

When to Use CC vs BCC:

Situation Use
Keeping your manager informed about a conversation CC your manager
Sending a job application and wanting your reference to know BCC your reference
Sending a group invitation to people who do not know each other BCC everyone (protects privacy)
Team discussion where everyone needs to see all replies CC the team

Key Points

  • Professional emails: clear subject, greeting, concise body, sign-off
  • Never use SMS language in professional emails
  • Set up an email signature for a professional touch
  • CC when others need to see the conversation, BCC to protect privacy

Topic 12: Google Drive Integration with Gmail

Explanation

When you need to send a file larger than 25 MB, or when you want to share a file that the recipient can view or edit online, use Google Drive.

Google Drive is Google's cloud storage service. Every Gmail account comes with 15 GB of free storage.

Step-by-Step Guide: Sharing Files via Google Drive in Gmail

Step 1: Upload to Google Drive (if not already there)
- Open a new tab and go to drive.google.com
- Click the + New button (top left)
- Click File upload
- Select your file and click Open
- Wait for the upload to finish

Step 2: Share via Gmail
- Go back to Gmail and click Compose
- At the bottom of the compose window, find the Google Drive icon (triangle shape)
- Click it
- Select the file you want to share
- Click Insert
- Gmail inserts a Drive link in your email

Step 3: Set Permissions
- Gmail may ask you to set sharing permissions
- Viewer: The recipient can only view the file (cannot edit)
- Commenter: The recipient can view and add comments
- Editor: The recipient can view and edit the file
- Choose the appropriate permission and click Send

Real-World Example

You need to send a 50 MB video of a school project to your teacher. Gmail's 25 MB limit will not allow a direct attachment. Instead, you upload the video to Google Drive, then share the Drive link in your email. Your teacher clicks the link and can watch the video directly in their browser.

Key Points

  • Google Drive gives you 15 GB of free cloud storage
  • Use Drive links to share files larger than 25 MB
  • You control who can view, comment, or edit your shared files
  • Drive links work better than attachments for large files or files you want to collaborate on
