Module 6: Tasks & Assignments -- Introduction to Internet & Email

How to Use This File

Complete each task in order. Check off each step as you finish it. If you get stuck, refer back to the content.md file for this module. Every task is designed to work on any Windows 11 computer with Google Chrome and an internet connection.

Task 1: Browser Navigation & Bookmarks

Difficulty: Beginner
Time: 15 minutes

Instructions

  1. Open Google Chrome from the Start Menu
  2. Navigate to each of these 5 websites by typing the URL in the address bar (do NOT search for them -- type the full address):
  3. www.wikipedia.org
  4. www.timeanddate.com
  5. www.weather.com
  6. news.google.com
  7. www.openstreetmap.org
  8. For each website, observe:
  9. Does it show the padlock icon (HTTPS)?
  10. What is the full URL in the address bar?
  11. Bookmark these 3 websites:
  12. wikipedia.org (Press Ctrl+D to bookmark)
  13. timeanddate.com
  14. news.google.com
  15. Show the bookmarks bar (Ctrl+Shift+B) and confirm all 3 bookmarks appear
  16. Open a new tab (Ctrl+T), close a tab (Ctrl+W), and reopen the closed tab (Ctrl+Shift+T)

Expected Result

  • You visited all 5 websites by typing URLs directly
  • 3 bookmarks are visible in your bookmarks bar
  • You can open, close, and reopen tabs using keyboard shortcuts

Bonus Challenge

Open all 5 websites in separate tabs at the same time. Practice switching between them using Ctrl+Tab. Right-click a tab and select "Pin tab" to see what pinning does.

Task 3: Create a Gmail Account

Difficulty: Beginner
Time: 20 minutes

Instructions

If you already have a Gmail account with a professional username, you may skip account creation and proceed to steps 5-7.

  1. Open Chrome and go to gmail.com
  2. Click Create account and select For my personal use
  3. Enter your real first and last name
  4. Choose a professional email address. Follow this format:
  5. Option 1: firstname.lastname (e.g., priya.sharma)
  6. Option 2: firstnamelastname (e.g., priyasharma)
  7. Option 3: firstname.lastname.number (e.g., priya.sharma42)
  8. Do NOT choose anything like coolgamer or funkyboy
  9. Create a strong password:
  10. At least 12 characters
  11. Includes uppercase, lowercase, numbers, and symbols
  12. Example format: Word$Word99!Word
  13. Write it down on paper and keep it safe
  14. Set up a profile picture:
  15. Click your profile icon (top-right circle)
  16. Click the camera icon or "Manage your Google Account"
  17. Go to Personal info and upload a photo (a simple, clear photo of yourself)
  18. Set up an email signature:
  19. Click the gear icon (top-right) and then "See all settings"
  20. Scroll down to "Signature"
  21. Click "Create new" and enter:
    Best regards, [Your Full Name] Phone: [Your Number]
  22. Click "Save Changes"

Expected Result

  • You have a Gmail account with a professional-sounding email address
  • Your account has a profile picture
  • Your signature is set up and will appear at the bottom of every email you send

Bonus Challenge

Explore your Gmail settings. Find where to change:
- The number of conversations per page (default is 50)
- Enable/disable the "Undo Send" feature and set it to 30 seconds
- Turn on keyboard shortcuts

Task 4: Send, Reply, and Attach

Difficulty: Beginner
Time: 20 minutes

Instructions

  1. Send an email to yourself:
  2. Click Compose
  3. In the "To" field, type YOUR OWN email address
  4. Subject: Test Email - Learning Gmail

  5. Body:
    ```
    Hello,

    This is my first test email. I am learning how to use Gmail
    as part of my Digital Skills Training course.

    I am practicing:
    - Composing emails
    - Formatting text
    - Adding attachments

    Best regards,
    [Your Name]
    ```
    - Make "first test email" bold using the formatting toolbar
    - Click Send

  6. Attach a file:

  7. Open Notepad (Start Menu, type "Notepad")
  8. Type a few lines about yourself
  9. Save the file as "about-me.txt" on your Desktop
  10. Go back to Gmail, click Compose
  11. To: your own email address
  12. Subject: Email with Attachment Test
  13. Body: "Please find my file attached."
  14. Click the paperclip icon at the bottom
  15. Navigate to Desktop, select "about-me.txt", click Open
  16. Confirm the file appears attached at the bottom of the email

  17. Click Send

  18. Reply to your email:

  19. Open the first email you sent to yourself (from your Inbox)
  20. Click Reply
  21. Type: "This is my reply. I have successfully learned to reply to emails."

  22. Click Send

  23. Download the attachment:

  24. Open the second email (with the attachment)
  25. Hover over the attached file
  26. Click the download icon (down arrow)
  27. Check your Downloads folder to confirm the file is there

Expected Result

  • Your Inbox contains two emails you sent to yourself
  • One email has a properly formatted body
  • One email has an attachment
  • You have a reply in your conversation thread
  • The attachment is saved in your Downloads folder

Bonus Challenge

Forward one of your test emails to a friend or family member. Add a message at the top explaining that you are practicing email skills.

Task 5: Real-Life Project -- Professional Job Application Email

Difficulty: Intermediate
Time: 30 minutes

Instructions

You will write and send a complete professional job application email. This is a real skill you will use throughout your career.

Step 1: Create a simple resume in Notepad
- Open Notepad
- Create a basic resume with this structure:
```
[YOUR FULL NAME]
[Your City, State]
[Your Phone Number]
[Your Email Address]

OBJECTIVE
[One sentence about the kind of job you are looking for]

EDUCATION
[Your highest qualification] - [Institution Name] - [Year]
[Previous qualification] - [Institution Name] - [Year]

SKILLS
- Microsoft Word (Basic)
- Microsoft Excel (Basic)
- Email Communication
- Typing Speed: [Your speed] WPM
- [Any other skills]

EXPERIENCE
[If you have any work experience, list it here]
[If not, write: "Fresher - eager to learn and contribute"]

LANGUAGES
[Languages you speak]
```
- Save the file as "Resume-YourName.txt" on your Desktop

Step 2: Compose the job application email
- Open Gmail and click Compose
- Fill in:
- To: Your own email address (since this is practice)
- CC: (leave empty for now)
- Subject: Application for Data Entry Position - [Your Full Name]
- Body:
```
Dear Hiring Manager,

I am writing to express my interest in the Data Entry Position 
advertised on your company website. I believe my skills and 
enthusiasm make me a strong candidate for this role.

I have recently completed my [Your Qualification] from 
[Your Institution] and have developed proficiency in:
- Microsoft Office (Word, Excel, PowerPoint)
- Email communication and professional correspondence
- Fast and accurate typing

I have attached my resume for your review and would welcome 
the opportunity to discuss how I can contribute to your team. 
I am available for an interview at your convenience.

Thank you for considering my application. I look forward to 
hearing from you.

Best regards,
[Your Full Name]
[Your Phone Number]
[Your Email Address]
```
  • Attach the resume file you created (paperclip icon, select file from Desktop)
  • Review everything one final time
  • Click Send

Step 3: Verify
- Open the sent email from your Inbox
- Check that the subject line is clear and professional
- Check that the body is well-formatted and error-free
- Check that the resume file is attached
- Check that your email signature appears at the bottom

Expected Result

  • A complete, professional job application email in your Sent folder
  • The email has: a clear subject, proper greeting, professional body, attachment, and sign-off
  • You could use this exact format when applying for real jobs

Bonus Challenge

Rewrite the same application email for a different job -- for example, a Customer Support Representative position. Change the subject line and customize the body to match the new role. Notice how you adapt the same professional format to different situations.

Module Assessment

Answer these questions to test your understanding. Write your answers on paper or in a Notepad file.

  1. What does HTTPS mean, and why is it important?
  2. Name three signs that a website might be fake or dangerous.
  3. What is the difference between CC and BCC in an email?
  4. What is the maximum attachment size for Gmail?
  5. You need to send a 40 MB video file via email. What should you do?
  6. Write a Google search query to find free PDF textbooks about Python programming.
  7. Why should you not do online banking on public WiFi?
  8. What makes a professional email address? Give one good and one bad example.
  9. What does "Reply All" do, and why should you be careful with it?
  10. How do you create a bookmark in Chrome? What keyboard shortcut is used?

Real-Life Mini Project

Scenario: You are organizing a small event (birthday party, study group, or community meeting). Use email to:

  1. Send an invitation email to at least 3 people (use your own email address 3 times if you do not have others to send to). Use BCC so recipients cannot see each other's email addresses.
  2. Include in the email: event name, date, time, location, and what to bring.
  3. Attach a simple "event schedule" file (create one in Notepad with times and activities).
  4. After sending, forward the invitation to one more person with a note: "Adding you to the guest list."
  5. Reply to the original email with an update: "Reminder: the event is tomorrow!"

This project combines: composing, formatting, BCC, attachments, forwarding, and replying -- all the email skills from this module.
