Topic 1: What is an Operating System?

Explanation

An operating system (OS) is the most important software on your computer. Without it, your computer is just a collection of metal and plastic that does nothing.

Think of it this way:

Analogy: The Restaurant Manager
Imagine a restaurant. The kitchen has ovens, pans, and ingredients (this is your hardware). The recipes are like your programs (software). But without a manager to coordinate everything -- take your order, tell the kitchen what to cook, make sure your food arrives at your table -- nothing would work.

The operating system IS that manager. It:
- Talks to the hardware (tells the screen what to show, tells the speakers what to play)
- Runs your programs (opens Word, Chrome, WhatsApp)
- Manages your files (keeps your documents, photos, and music organised)
- Handles security (blocks viruses, requires passwords)

Windows 11 is the operating system we will learn. It is made by Microsoft and is the most widely used OS on desktop and laptop computers worldwide.

Other operating systems exist:
- macOS -- used on Apple Mac computers
- Linux -- used by advanced users and servers
- Android -- used on most smartphones
- iOS -- used on iPhones and iPads

For this course, we focus entirely on Windows 11.

Real-World Example

When you double-click on a Word document, Windows does all of this:
1. Finds the file on your hard drive
2. Tells the CPU to start Microsoft Word
3. Loads the file into RAM
4. Shows the document on your screen
5. Waits for your keyboard input

All of this happens in seconds, and the operating system manages every step.

Key Points to Remember

  • The operating system manages everything on your computer
  • Windows 11 is the OS we use in this course
  • Without an OS, hardware cannot do anything useful
  • The OS runs programs, manages files, and handles security

Topic 2: The Windows 11 Desktop

Explanation

When your computer finishes starting up, the first thing you see is the desktop. This is your main workspace -- like the top of a physical desk.

What you see on the desktop:

  1. Wallpaper (Background): The picture that covers your screen. This is purely decorative -- you can change it to any picture you like.

  2. Icons: Small pictures on the desktop that represent files, folders, or shortcuts to programs. Double-click an icon to open it.

  3. Taskbar: The bar at the very bottom of the screen. This is the most important part of your desktop. It is always visible and contains:

  4. Start button (Windows logo, centre) -- opens the Start Menu
  5. Search icon (magnifying glass) -- search for anything
  6. Pinned apps -- apps you use often, always available with one click
  7. Running apps -- apps currently open (shown with a line underneath)

  8. System tray (far right) -- clock, WiFi, battery, volume, notifications

  9. System Tray (Notification Area): The right side of the taskbar. Shows:

  10. Current time and date
  11. WiFi/network status
  12. Battery level (on laptops)
  13. Volume control
  14. Small icons for background programs (antivirus, etc.)

Step-by-Step Guide: Exploring the Desktop

  1. Look at the bottom of your screen. The horizontal bar is the taskbar.
  2. Find the Windows logo in the centre of the taskbar. This is the Start button.
  3. Look at the far right of the taskbar. You should see the time, a WiFi icon, a speaker icon, and a battery icon (if on a laptop).
  4. Look at the main screen area above the taskbar. This is the desktop. Any icons you see here are shortcuts.
  5. Try moving your mouse cursor (the arrow) around the screen. Notice how it moves as you move the mouse or your finger on the touchpad.

Key Points to Remember

  • The desktop is your main workspace
  • The taskbar is at the bottom and always visible
  • The Start button is in the centre of the taskbar
  • The system tray (right side) shows time, WiFi, battery, and volume

Topic 3: The Taskbar

Explanation

The taskbar is the bar at the bottom of your screen. It is one of the most useful features of Windows because it gives you quick access to everything.

Parts of the taskbar from left to right:
- Start button -- opens the Start Menu (where you find all programs)
- Search -- type the name of anything to find it instantly
- Pinned apps -- apps you have pinned for quick access (like a shortcut)
- Running apps -- apps that are currently open (small line underneath the icon)
- System tray -- clock, WiFi, volume, battery, notifications

How to Pin an App to the Taskbar:
1. Click the Start button.
2. Find the app you want (search for it or look in "All apps").
3. Right-click on the app.
4. Click "Pin to taskbar."
5. The app now appears on your taskbar permanently.

How to Unpin an App from the Taskbar:
1. Find the app on the taskbar.
2. Right-click on it.
3. Click "Unpin from taskbar."
4. The app disappears from the taskbar (but it is not deleted -- you can still find it in the Start Menu).

Real-World Example

Think of the taskbar like the top of your physical desk. You keep your most-used items within arm's reach -- your pen, phone, and notebook. The taskbar does the same: your most-used apps are always one click away.

Key Points to Remember

  • Right-click an app to pin or unpin it from the taskbar
  • Pinning does not install or delete anything -- it just creates a shortcut
  • A running app shows a small line under its icon
  • You can rearrange pinned apps by dragging them left or right

Topic 4: The Start Menu

Explanation

The Start Menu is the gateway to everything on your computer. Click the Windows icon in the centre of the taskbar (or press the Windows key on your keyboard) to open it.

What you see in the Start Menu:

  1. Search bar at the top -- type any app name, file name, or setting to find it instantly. This is the fastest way to find anything.

  2. Pinned apps -- a grid of apps you use often. You can add or remove apps here.

  3. Recommended -- recently opened files and newly installed apps. Windows shows these to help you quickly get back to what you were doing.

  4. All apps button -- click this to see every program installed on your computer, listed alphabetically.

  5. Your account and Power button at the bottom.

Step-by-Step Guide: Using the Start Menu

  1. Click the Start button (Windows icon in the taskbar) or press the Windows key on your keyboard.
  2. The Start Menu opens. You see pinned apps at the top and recommended files below.
  3. To search for an app: Just start typing. For example, type "calculator" and it appears in the search results. Click on it to open.
  4. To see all apps: Click "All apps" in the top-right area of the Start Menu. Scroll down through the alphabetical list.
  5. To pin an app to Start Menu: In the All Apps list, right-click an app and select "Pin to Start."
  6. To unpin from Start Menu: Right-click a pinned app and select "Unpin from Start."
  7. To close the Start Menu: click anywhere outside it, or press the Windows key again.

Key Points to Remember

  • Press the Windows key or click the Start button to open the Start Menu
  • The fastest way to find anything is to type its name in the search bar
  • "All apps" shows every program on your computer
  • You can pin your favourite apps and unpin ones you do not use

Topic 5: File Explorer -- Navigating Your Files

Explanation

File Explorer is the app you use to find, open, and manage all files and folders on your computer. Think of it as the filing cabinet of your computer.

How to open File Explorer:
- Click the folder icon on the taskbar, OR
- Press Win+E on your keyboard (Windows key + E)

What you see when File Explorer opens:

  • Navigation pane (left side): Shows main locations:
  • Quick access / Home: Your most-used folders
  • Desktop: Files and folders on your desktop
  • Downloads: Files you downloaded from the internet
  • Documents: Your personal documents
  • Pictures: Your photos and images

  • This PC: Shows all drives (C:, D:, USB drives)

  • Address bar (top): Shows where you currently are. Example: "This PC > Documents > My Work"

  • Main area (centre/right): Shows the files and folders in your current location.

  • Back and Forward buttons (top-left): Go back to the previous folder or forward again.

  • Search box (top-right): Search for files within the current folder.

Step-by-Step Guide: Navigating to Key Folders

  1. Press Win+E to open File Explorer.
  2. In the left sidebar, click "Desktop." You will see files that are on your desktop.
  3. Click the Back arrow (top-left) to go back.
  4. Click "Documents" in the left sidebar. This is where you should save most of your work.
  5. Click "Downloads." This is where files from the internet are saved.
  6. Click "Pictures." This is for photos and images.
  7. Click "This PC." Here you can see your hard drive (usually "Local Disk C:") and any USB drives.

Real-World Example

When you download a job application form from the internet, it goes to the Downloads folder. You can then move it to your Documents folder to keep things organised. If someone emails you photos, they go to Downloads too, and you can move them to Pictures.

Key Points to Remember

  • Open File Explorer with Win+E or the folder icon on the taskbar
  • Key folders: Desktop, Documents, Downloads, Pictures
  • The left sidebar lets you jump to any location quickly
  • The address bar at the top shows where you are

Topic 6: Creating Folders and Files

Explanation

Keeping your files organised is one of the most important computer skills. Folders are like physical folders in a filing cabinet -- they hold related files together.

Step-by-Step Guide: Creating a New Folder

  1. Open File Explorer (Win+E).
  2. Navigate to where you want the new folder (for example, click "Documents").
  3. Right-click on an empty space in the main area (not on an existing file).
  4. A menu appears. Hover over "New" at the top.
  5. Click "Folder."
  6. A new folder appears with its name highlighted in blue, ready to be typed.
  7. Type the name you want (for example, "My Schoolwork") and press Enter.
  8. Your folder is created. Double-click it to go inside.

Naming rules for folders:
- Use clear, descriptive names (good: "2024 Invoices," bad: "Stuff")
- Do not use these special characters: \ / : * ? " < > |
- Keep names reasonably short

Step-by-Step Guide: Creating a New Text File

  1. Open File Explorer and navigate to the folder where you want the file.
  2. Right-click on an empty space.
  3. Hover over "New."
  4. Click "Text Document."
  5. Type a name for the file (for example, "My Notes") and press Enter.
  6. Double-click the file to open it. It opens in Notepad.
  7. Type some text inside.
  8. Press Ctrl+S to save.
  9. Close Notepad by clicking the X in the top-right corner.

Step-by-Step Guide: Renaming Files and Folders

Method 1: Right-click
1. Right-click on the file or folder.
2. Click "Rename" (or look for the rename icon).
3. The name becomes editable. Type the new name.
4. Press Enter.

Method 2: Keyboard shortcut
1. Click on the file or folder once to select it (it becomes highlighted).
2. Press the F2 key on your keyboard.
3. The name becomes editable. Type the new name.
4. Press Enter.

Step-by-Step Guide: Deleting Files and Folders

  1. Click on the file or folder to select it.
  2. Press the Delete key on your keyboard, OR right-click and select "Delete."
  3. The file moves to the Recycle Bin (it is not gone yet -- you can recover it).
  4. To permanently delete: open the Recycle Bin on the desktop, right-click the file, and select "Delete" (or click "Empty Recycle Bin" to delete everything in it).

Key Points to Remember

  • Right-click on empty space to create new folders and files
  • Give folders clear, descriptive names
  • F2 is the quick way to rename
  • Deleted files go to the Recycle Bin first -- you can recover them
  • Ctrl+S saves your work -- use it constantly

Topic 7: Opening and Closing Applications

Explanation

An application (also called a program or app) is software that lets you do a specific task. Here is how to open and close them.

Step-by-Step Guide: Opening Applications

Method 1: Start Menu Search (Fastest)
1. Press the Windows key on your keyboard.
2. Start typing the name of the app (e.g., "Notepad").
3. Click the app when it appears in the search results.

Method 2: Taskbar
1. If the app is pinned to your taskbar, click its icon once.

Method 3: Desktop Shortcut
1. If the app has an icon on your desktop, double-click it.

Method 4: Start Menu All Apps
1. Click the Start button.
2. Click "All apps."
3. Scroll to find the app alphabetically and click it.

Step-by-Step Guide: Closing Applications

Method 1: X Button
1. Look at the top-right corner of the app window.
2. Click the X button (it turns red when you hover over it).

Method 2: Keyboard Shortcut
1. Make sure the app is the active window (click on it if needed).
2. Press Alt+F4. The app closes.

Important: If you have unsaved work, the app will ask "Do you want to save?" Click "Save" to keep your work, or "Don't Save" to discard changes.

Key Points to Remember

  • The fastest way to open an app is to press the Windows key and type its name
  • Close apps with the X button or Alt+F4
  • Always save your work before closing (Ctrl+S)
  • If an app asks to save, click "Save" unless you want to discard your changes

Topic 8: Sleep, Restart, and Shut Down

Explanation

There are three ways to stop using your computer. Each one does something different.

Mode What Happens When to Use
Sleep The screen turns off and the computer uses very little power, but everything stays in memory. When you open the lid or press a key, it wakes up in seconds with everything exactly as you left it. Taking a short break (lunch, overnight).
Restart The computer shuts down completely and turns back on automatically. All programs close, RAM is cleared, and Windows starts fresh. When something is not working right, or after installing updates.
Shut Down The computer turns off completely. Everything closes and power is cut. Next time you press the power button, it starts from scratch. When you will not use the computer for a long time, or when travelling.

Step-by-Step Guide: Using Sleep, Restart, and Shut Down

  1. Click the Start button (Windows icon in the taskbar).
  2. Click the Power icon (bottom-right of the Start Menu).
  3. Choose one:
  4. Sleep -- screen goes dark, wakes up quickly later
  5. Restart -- shuts down and turns on again automatically
  6. Shut down -- turns off completely

Quick Sleep: On most laptops, simply closing the lid puts it to sleep.

Real-World Example

  • Sleep: You are going to lunch. Close the laptop lid. When you come back, open it, and everything is exactly as you left it.
  • Restart: Your WiFi is not working properly. Restart the computer -- this often fixes temporary problems.
  • Shut Down: You are done for the day and putting the laptop in your bag.

Key Points to Remember

  • Sleep = quick nap (everything stays in memory, resumes fast)
  • Restart = fixes problems (clears everything and starts fresh)
  • Shut Down = fully off (for when you are done or travelling)
  • Closing the laptop lid usually puts it to sleep

Topic 9: Connecting to WiFi

Explanation

WiFi is wireless internet. To browse the internet, check email, or use online apps, you need to be connected to a WiFi network.

Step-by-Step Guide: Connecting to WiFi

  1. Look at the bottom-right corner of the taskbar. Find the WiFi icon (it looks like a fan or signal bars). If you see a small globe icon instead, you are not connected.
  2. Click the WiFi icon (or the globe). A panel opens showing available networks.
  3. If WiFi is off, click the WiFi button to turn it on.
  4. You will see a list of available networks. Find yours (this is usually the name of your router or the network name given to you by your internet provider).
  5. Click on your network name.
  6. Check the box that says "Connect automatically" (so you do not have to do this every time).
  7. Click "Connect."
  8. If the network is password-protected (most are), type the WiFi password and click "Next."
  9. Wait a few seconds. The WiFi icon should now show signal bars, meaning you are connected.

Where to find your WiFi password:
- On the bottom or back of your WiFi router (small box from your internet provider)
- On the paperwork from your internet provider
- Ask whoever set up the internet in your home or office

Key Points to Remember

  • Click the WiFi icon in the bottom-right to see available networks
  • Select your network, enter the password, and click Connect
  • Tick "Connect automatically" so you do not have to repeat this
  • No WiFi icon? Your WiFi might be off -- look for a WiFi toggle button

Topic 10: Windows Updates

Explanation

Windows Updates are improvements and security fixes that Microsoft releases regularly. They fix problems, add new features, and protect your computer from viruses and hackers.

Why updates matter:
- Security: Updates fix weaknesses that hackers could exploit
- Stability: Updates fix bugs that cause crashes or slow performance
- Features: Updates sometimes add new useful features

Step-by-Step Guide: Checking for Updates

  1. Click the Start button.
  2. Type "Windows Update" and click on "Windows Update settings."
  3. Click "Check for updates."
  4. If updates are available, click "Download & install."
  5. Some updates require a restart. If prompted, save your work and click "Restart now."

Key Points to Remember

  • Keep Windows updated for security and performance
  • Check for updates at least once a week
  • Some updates require restarting your computer
  • Never ignore security updates

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  1. Not using the Search function. Beginners often scroll through menus looking for apps. Just press the Windows key and type the name -- it is much faster.
  2. Saving files to random locations. Always save to Documents, Desktop, or a folder you created. If you save files without paying attention to the location, you will lose them.
  3. Never renaming downloaded files. Files you download often have confusing names like "document(1)(2).pdf." Rename them immediately to something clear.
  4. Forgetting to save. Press Ctrl+S constantly. Every few minutes when working on a document.
  5. Thinking delete means gone forever. Files go to the Recycle Bin first. You can get them back (until you empty the Recycle Bin).
  6. Ignoring Windows Updates. Updates keep you safe. Install them weekly.
  7. Confusing Sleep and Shut Down. Sleep is fine for short breaks. Shut Down when travelling or not using the computer for days.
